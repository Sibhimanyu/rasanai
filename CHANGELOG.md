# Changelog

## 1.14.0 (unreleased)

- **Launch films are about the viewer's day.** A measured comparison of the Kinso launch with our ChatGPT film: Kinso's story is the viewer's ("Your next deal is in here somewhere", "Woah", "You are a really busy person", the reveal at 27%, three benefits each with one UI behaviour, "Join 27,300 others on the waitlist", the logo and URL; 27 lines in 46 s), ours was the product's ("Answers you can drag", no "you", the reveal at 12%, a weak CTA; 7 lines in 30 s). A new launch shape `pas` (pain, meet, benefits, proof, cta) sits beside the ladder, and `story.mjs pick --format launch` offers both across Sure, Bold and Wild (`--shape` forces one).
- **Gate G12, "viewer first".** On launch and promo films, errors for no line addressing the viewer in the first 25% (`not-viewer-first`), a pas reveal before 20% (`reveal-too-early`), a benefit or rung with no `benefit` in viewer language (`no-benefit`), no proof when the claims have a number (`no-proof`), a CTA without an action and a place (`weak-cta`), and more than 3.0 s per on-screen line (`sparse-lines`); warnings for `slogan-copy` and `toy-demos`. Pitch fields: `benefit` per benefit or rung beat, `proof: {line, claim}`, `cta: {line, action, where}`.
- **Docs and prompts follow.** `script.md` (pass 0 starts from the viewer's day, a worked pas for a non-Google product with Kinso-like rows, the conversational-copy rules: second person, reactions, a line every 1.5 to 2.5 s), `story.md`, `product-first.md` (rule 3), `launch-film.md` (pas templates by length), the script writer, the script editor (a product-centric line is the note) and the concept critic ("is it about the viewer's day?").
- **Sound is part of every film.** The direct path never ran `sound.mjs sfx-plan`, so films shipped with no SFX and a flat library bed (LRA 1.6 LU against Kinso's 3.0). SKILL.md's direct-path build step and section 4b step 3 now apply to every route; the film builder's `## Events` lists every causal event and every transition with its time. `sound.mjs check` errors `no-sfx` (a film of 15 s or more with no SFX cues, unless `--silent-by-choice`) and warns `flat-dynamics` and `ring-out-ending`.
- **The music follows the story.** The score carries `music_arc: { intro_until, drop_at, break, payoff_at, button_at }` (`crew.mjs` warns `music-arc-missing`); the Motion Director aligns the reveal with the drop and the logo with the button and passes them to `sound.mjs fit --reveal --logo`; every seam that moves carries a sound event (`seam-unsounded`). `sound.md` gains "The music arc" and the SFX rules for transitions, landings, taps, counters and the reveal; the user's own track first, and a generated track to the arc through the `higgsfield` CLI (`seed_audio`) as an option.
- **A flat brand is not a white void.** A flat brand bans only what its own films never do. Mood grounds (the ground changes on the story's emotional turns within the palette or the film's colour fields) and depth blur (a defocused word behind the UI, motion blur on fast moves) are allowed unless the card says otherwise. The brand film analyst writes `Mood grounds:` and `Depth blur:` lines from what the films do (OpenAI's "Refreshed." uses colour fields, coloured dot fields and photography); `brand-film.md`, `launch-film.md` rule 9, `craft.md`, `3d.md` (Brand-flat 3D allows `fstop` when the card allows depth blur), the Motion Director and the film builder follow; `crew.mjs` `flat-3d-style` accepts `fstop` and motion blur under an allowing card and still bans bloom, glow, grain, chromatic aberration, chrome and glass.
- **Motion variety and punctuation.** Kinso runs about 20 distinct mechanics, 12 punctuation cuts and huge-against-tiny type; ours had about 7 mechanics, 1 to 2 cuts and one type size. The score records `mechanic` per shot, and `crew.mjs` warns `mechanic-repeat` (3 consecutive lines), `mechanics-thin` (fewer than min(lines, 12) on films of 25 s or more), `flat-type` (`type_scale` large/small under 3) and `no-punctuation` (no hard cut on an emotional turn). `craft.md` gains "Variety and punctuation"; the Motion Director and film builder follow.
- **Readability allowance.** An oversize word may sweep across the frame cropped for up to 1.2 s (Kinso's "for every") when the same line then rests readable: `text-fit` raises the push-through allowance from 0.4 s to 1.2 s, and the docs say so.
- Why: set next to Kinso's launch, our ChatGPT film talked about itself, sounded flat, looked like a white page and moved the same way all the way through. RasanAI told the product's story, left the sound to a library loop, read a brand's flatness as no mood and no blur, and used one mechanic and one type size.

## 1.13.0 (unreleased)

- **3D is a normal technique, for any show.** The references use real 3D (the Claude reel's chrome blobs and particle ring, mexicat's wireframe library and black hole, Google's tilted glowing panels, OpenAI's rotating sphere of dots) and RasanAI had suppressed Rasan3D: a flat-looking brand card became "no 3D at all". Now 3D is used wherever it makes a line stronger (a line's meaning in space), still serving a line, keeping every word readable and landing on the real product in a product-first film.
- **A flat brand restricts the style of 3D, not its use.** `references/3d.md` gains "Brand-flat 3D": matte or unlit materials (basic, toon, flat, matte, unlit, lambert) in the palette, clean even light, no bloom, glow, grain, chromatic aberration or lens flare, depth of field and motion blur only if the card allows blur, with the dot-sphere and tilted-panel recipes as Rasan3D code. The brand film analyst writes a "3D style:" line instead of "no 3D"; `brand-film.md`, `launch-film.md` rule 9, the Motion Director, the film builder and `craft.md` follow.
- **Ambition floor and gates.** Films of 20 s or more need at least one `3d` or `hybrid` scene (or a 3D shot inside a hybrid scene) unless `depth.none_because` quotes the script line(s) 3D would hurt (`no-3d-moment`); on a flat brand, 3D scenes must declare flat `materials` and no `fstop`/blur the card forbids (`flat-3d-style`). Unbranded films keep the old `no-3d` rule.
- **Grammars get 3D techniques.** Every grammar gains 2 to 3 Rasan3D techniques (editorial grid: cells flip as 3D cards, the grid tilts into a receding plane; specimen sheet: the specimen turns on its label axis, a trace lifts into a wireframe; dot system: the dot sphere (Refreshed.) and its collapse; glyph chain: a letter extrudes and turns to reveal its counter; glow panels, viewfinder reel), `space: "3d"` and `flat_ok` per technique.
- **Showcases mark their 3D.** Line rows that are real 3D in the references carry `"space": "3d"`, and the README adds the principle "Space is a technique".
- **Every line has a show.** The motion should tell the story: in ten reference films (`library/showcases/`, broken down line by line in text) each on-screen line has a motion that means it, built from the product's UI or the film's one actor (Sovra's "Same songs" pill swaps songs, feeds, loops and swells into rings; Kinso's notification stack splits "somewhere"), with words and UI touching and lines changing in place. Each beat in a pitch now carries `shows: [{line, show, built_from, handoff}]`, one row per on-screen line, and the pitch an `actor: {what, does}` (required on launch, promo and brand films).
- **Gate G11, "every line shows".** `story.mjs check` errors (warnings outside launch, promo and brand films) on a beat with words and no shows, a row without `show` or `built_from`, a label-only or under-8-word show, an unbound show that would fit any line (`show-unbound`), a missing handoff, and on product-first films fewer than 60% of rows from product UI or the actor (`show-off-product`). It warns when no row has words and UI touching (`no-touch`) or none changes in place (`no-swap`).
- **The pipeline realises the shows.** `script.md` gains the pass "The show column" (the ten principles, real Sovra and Kinso rows, one bad example and its fix); the writer, the editor (a caption over UI is the note), the move inventor (a card for every show, at least one per beat, heroes stay 2 or 3; `show-uncarded`), the Motion Director (every row realised in a shot; `show-unscored`, an error above 30% unscored) and the film builder (the shows are the build target per line) follow, and the script writer, move inventor and Motion Director are given the showcases README and two films for the film's kind.
- **The Story screen shows the shows.** The script table's "What we see" column becomes "What the motion does": each beat lists its show rows (line, then what the motion does), in the console and natively in Studio.
- Why: the user's note, "the motion should tell the story". RasanAI planned two or three hero moves at the joins and left every other line a caption.
- **Motion grammars.** A film commits to ONE grammar: a frame device that moves on the beat, fixed type and image behaviour, and 8 to 12 techniques with build routes. Six are seeded in `library/grammars/` (`editorial-grid`, `glyph-chain`, `viewfinder-reel`, `specimen-sheet`, `glow-panels`, `dot-system`), distilled from references described in text only; `viewfinder-reel` and `glow-panels` are `flat_ok: false` (chrome, particles, glow), so a flat brand film only gets the other four. `scripts/grammar.mjs` lists, shows, picks (story shape and brand constraints), writes (into the run, merged with the brand palette) and checks them.
- **Where the grammar binds.** Each look is a design system plus one grammar (`design/<L>/grammar.json`; the three looks use three different ones) and the specimen is a 4 to 5 s moving sketch of the story's open and first rung in it. The direct branded path picks and writes a grammar and records it in the `look` decision. The Motion Director names `score.grammar` and, on every beat, a `technique` and a `frame_device` (`crew.mjs check`: `grammar-missing`, `technique-unknown`, `technique-repeat`, `frame-device-missing`, warning `technique-thin`); the film builder and scene animators inline the techniques; the move inventor reads the grammar when it exists; `craft.md` gains "Grammar: one per film, a technique per beat".
- **Launch films tell a ladder.** A new pitch field `shape: "ladder" | "scenario"`. A product or brand launch is a ladder by default: one refrain verb is the spine ("Ask simple questions", "Ask longer", "Ask it to do the research", "Ask anything"), a title card per rung with one highlighted word, each rung a real UI demo of a different everyday use, escalating from simple to done-for-you, then the close and the logo. A single-feature spot stays a `scenario` (one viewer, one task, today's rules). Rung counts by length: 3 to 4 up to 35 s, 4 to 6 to 60 s, 6 to 8 beyond.
- **G10 is shape-aware.** A ladder fails on a missing refrain verb, a title without it, a rung count out of range, two rungs sharing a use noun, levels that do not rise, a rung over 35% of the film, a rung without UI cause and effect, or no close. `story.mjs pick --format launch` offers the ladder as the structure for all three; Sure, Bold and Wild differ in uses, order, pace and the refrain's wording, never in being one plot.
- **Docs and prompts follow.** SKILL.md rule 15 and the Story section, `product-first.md` rule 3, `script.md` (a Folio notes-app ladder worked example), `story.md` (Story shapes), `launch-film.md` (ladder templates by length), the script writer, the script editor (reads the ladder) and the concept critic's question 7 (the film's shape).
- **Readability is a hard rule.** Every line meant to be read rests fully inside the 6% safe area, on one line (two at most), at the brand film card's type scale, never cropped; it may leave the frame only in a push-through of 0.4 s or less. Every ask for huge or edge-cropping type is gone from the library bar (`bar.json`), the move inventor and juror, `moves.md`, the writer, `script.md`, the Motion Director, the film builder and `craft.md` (§5 composition, §9 tells, §10 gates). `scale: "full-frame"` now means the transition fills the frame while the words stay readable.
- **Legibility gate for moves.** A hero must read on first watch. The juror gets binary gate G6 (legible): it fails hidden-letter puzzles (a dot, a missing letter) that stop the line reading, and any move that needs explaining.
- **Moves serve the ladder.** On a ladder the heroes are the joins between rungs (a title word becomes the input, the input the next demo, the last input the logo); the carrier travels across rungs and nothing invents plot inside a demo. The moves check fails a ladder with no hero seam across a rung boundary (`no-join-hero`).
- **`text-fit` check.** The built composition is sampled headlessly at 10 fps; a visible text element (opacity above 0.5, 20 px or larger) that crosses the frame edge, or at rest leaves the 6% safe area, for more than 0.4 s continuously is the error `text-cropped` (time range and first 40 characters). It runs in `motion-gate.mjs --project` and `slop.mjs --project`.
- Why: a real test film (a ChatGPT launch) followed one dinner bill for 30 s ("that's not how launch videos are made": Google's "Ask Search Anything" is a refrain ladder of different everyday uses) and set its type huge and cropped at both edges ("y of five", "clipb"), because 1.12 asked for full-frame type and hidden-letter tricks read as random.

## 1.12.0 (unreleased)

- **Moves reach the bar.** The reference clip ships as the house bar (`library/moves/bar.json`): a frame-level textual breakdown (nine ledger rows, four move cards, density 0.82 ideas per second, full-frame scale, six lessons), no frames and no third-party pixels. `moves.mjs pack` puts it first in every pack as `bar`, never counted among the exemplars, with the rule "the bar, never the content". The inventor reads it before anything and matches its density and scale.
- **Product-first allows transformations that land on the product.** A letter, shape or type transformation, up to full frame, is craft when its end state lands on a real UI element, state or screen, or the real brand mark or name, within the same move; invented worlds, museums, metaphors and any transformation that never resolves stay banned. Moves carry `resolves_to` (required on a product-first hero); `origin` stays required but no longer has to name a product word. The inventor and juror prompts give one allowed and one banned example, and the juror's G3 passes a transformation that resolves.
- **Scale and density are enforced.** Every card has `scale` (`full-frame`, `large`, `detail`); a moves file with no full-frame type or shape hero fails (`no-full-frame-hero`); `chain` (every transformation of the carrier, time-coded) must have `ceil(film_seconds / 1.5)` entries (`chain-sparse`) and no gap over 3 s (`chain-gap`). The juror's new set gate S1 denies a set with no full-frame type or shape transformation even if three cards pass. A carrier's chain counts as ONE motion type and ONE idea for the "one signature" rule (craft §4, launch-film rule 9); the chain is the Motion Director's score spine.
- **Brand restraint is not calm.** "One element at a time" now means one carrier at a time, transformed in a chain. The brand film analyst writes the brand's morph chain (OpenAI's "Refreshed.": dot -> rings -> dot grid -> letterforms -> mark) into FILM-STYLE.md, and the inventor derives its carrier and chain from it.
- **Writers write lines worth transforming.** On-screen lines are the stage, not captions: short lines whose letters, dots and full stops can become the product, with room for the type to be huge.
- **Three scripts, three kinds of carrier.** In a real test all three inventors (parallel, blind to each other) chose "a dot", so the three stories felt alike. `moves.mjs pack` now assigns each label a different carrier family (`glyph`, `component`, `line`, `object`, `mark`, `data`), drawn deterministically from hash(run basename + seed), with a description and examples. `carrier.family` is required and must be the pack's (`carrier-family`); `moves.mjs check-set --run R`, also run by `check-verdict` and the crew's juror check, errors `carrier-repeat` when two scripts share a family and warns `carrier-similar` when two carrier names share a content word. A brand motif (the brand film card or DESIGN.md) is noted in the pack as `brand_motif`: secondary anywhere, the carrier only for `glyph` or `mark`.
- **The juror's pairwise rounds are checkable.** The verdict carries `pitches.<L>.rounds` (`{a, b, first, winner, frame}`): every consecutive pair of the ranking in both presentation orders, each with a deciding frame of 8 words or more. `rounds-missing`, `rounds-inconsistent` and `rounds-frame` fail the check; a split pair needs `tiebreak: "riskier"` on the second round and the riskier card ranked higher. The juror had admitted ranking "mentally" and nothing could tell.
- Why: a real test (a 30 s ChatGPT launch film) produced moves that were clever but tiny, hidden inside the UI (a thinking dot became a 20 px clip rivet), one per beat. Product-first treated every letter or shape transformation as a conceit and the juror failed a full-frame shape idea as an invented world; the reference clip never reached the inventors; and "one element at a time" was read as "be calm" when the brand's own film is a morph chain.

- **Product-first and brand films keep their rules.** On a launch film the carrier must be a real product surface or the brand glyph, glyph puns resolve within a second and the seed is a choreography rule, not a metaphor; on a branded film the mechanisms come only from its motion vocabulary and the ambition goes into the carrier and the choreography.

- Why: the show-off push was not enough. The plan named techniques ("match-cut", "iris") instead of ideas because motion was first planned after the user had chosen the story, the score asked for labels and never for the object that crosses the cut, nothing overgenerated and selected, and one agent graded its own showreel. The reference was a clip where one circle travels from the "o" of "Your" to a cat's pupil to the next page.

## 1.10.0 (2026-10-10)

- **Films that keep moving.** A short film (45 s or less, or a single-feature launch) is now written by one film builder in one pass, so objects carry across beats and no seam comes to rest. Longer films, or `--deep`, keep parallel animators with a lead builder owning everything that crosses a cut.
- **A motion gate on every draft** (`scripts/motion-gate.mjs`): visible motion in at least 75% of frames, no freeze of 0.8 s, an end card still for at most 1.5 s, no slow creeping zoom, motion on both sides of every seam, at least two seams carried by an object, one brand reveal. Critics need its report. HyperFrames' own launch film passes it; RasanAI's previous draft did not.
- **No scheduled holds.** Something meaningful changes every 1.5 to 2.5 s; holds are reading time with secondary motion. Library cards and taxonomy that asked for stillness were rewritten.
- **One feature, one scenario.** Launch films name a viewer, their task, the before and the after; beats are hook, proof, turn and call to action, each with the real on-screen cause and effect. Feature tours, metaphors and invented screens fail the new story gate G10 and the concept critic.
- **Stories say what they achieve.** Writers decide the aim first (the takeaway, the feeling, the next action, the audience); titles name the idea (gate G8).
- **Faster tempo.** House tempo for launch films (at least 5 ideas in 30 s, a change every 1.5 to 2.5 s, short holds), the brand film's measured tempo when there is one, and gate G9.
- **Reference moments.** `scripts/moments.mjs` searches a credited catalogue of real launch-film moments by role and technique and fetches the picked ones for the run; the builder takes one mechanic from each and builds it fresh. Nothing from a moment ships; creators are credited.
- **One plan file.** `motion/score.json` carries the film and every beat; the other build documents derive from it, so the builder reads about 19 KB instead of hundreds.
- **Shorter path for short films:** no key frames, no look desk when a brand applies (the look is the brand's, decided with a reason), lean research. The human calls stay; the Animatic shows the built draft.
- **Real logo files only.** Research downloads the official logo with its source; the build places that exact file; a check fails drawn or substituted logos. Without an official logo the end card uses the name in type and says so.
- **Motion levels** cover how intricate the movement is, not only how many elements move.
- Self-test: all checks pass.

## 1.9.0 (2026-10-09)

- **Brand films are researched from the brand's own films.** For a branded launch, promo or brand film, a new `brand-film` phase finds the brand's official films (`scripts/brandfilm.mjs find`, via yt-dlp), or uses a video the user attached as the primary reference. It downloads at most 2 (720p), extracts scene-cut frames and contact sheets, and measures the film: mean and background luminance, white and black share, palette with shares, neutral versus chromatic colour, colour moments and cut rate (`frames`, `measure`, `card`). A Sonnet brand film analyst (`agents/brand-film-analyst.md`) reads the sheets and fills `FILM-STYLE.md`: canvas, palette, typefaces and type scale, layout, signature motif, motion vocabulary, photography, cut rate, transitions and end card. About 3 minutes on Fast pace; it falls back to the website when no film exists.
- **Looks come from that card, not from outside references.** For branded films, Sure is the brand's grammar exactly and Bold and Wild vary only composition and pacing. `lib/system.mjs` requires a `film_style` citation, keeps canvas, ink, accent and display face on the card, rejects 3D, dolly, blur and grain when the card is flat, and forbids outside library references; `look-payload` refuses off-card looks.
- **Simple, to-the-point launch stories** (`references/launch-film.md`, a researched rulebook). Fixed beat templates for 15, 30, 60 and 90 s: the brand or product within 1 to 3 s, the reveal, 2 to 4 real demos, a payoff line and the end card. The three scripts differ only in emphasis and order. `story.mjs` G7 checks beat roles and timings and fails any beat without the product or brand.
- **Style-match gate.** After key frames and after the first draft, `brandfilm.mjs compare` measures ours against the brand film: luminance, white and black share, hue-aware off-brand colour, neutral share, colour moments and cut rate. A fail is fixed before the film continues, and the numbers are shown to the user.
- **Motion obeys the brand.** The Motion Director reads `FILM-STYLE.md` first; a flat card means no 3D, blur or grain.
- Why: the ChatGPT for Mac test film was about 46% dark green with a Bodoni serif and a museum metaphor. OpenAI's own "Refreshed." film is about 77% white canvas with OpenAI Sans and a dot motif. Calibrated on that pair, the gate passes the reference against itself and fails our film on 7 of 11 checks (58% off-brand hue). Self-test: all checks pass.

## 1.8.0 (2026-10-09)

- **Launch, promo and product films are product-first** (`references/product-first.md`). The product's real UI is on screen within 3 s; there is one hero moment showing the key feature for real, 2 to 4 real uses, on-screen lines of six words or fewer, and the required end line set as the largest type. Conceit devices (museums, allegories, invented worlds, extended metaphors) are filtered out of `story.mjs pick` for these routes (`--allow-conceit` opts out), and the new gate G7 in `story.mjs check` fails them unless they are an instant visual pun. All three scripts stay product-led; Sure, Bold and Wild differ in structure and energy, not in leaving the product. Show-off means craft (UI choreography, rhythm, motion), not concept.
- **Clearest story recommended.** On these routes the editor recommends the clearest product story and must say why a first-time viewer gets it in one watch (`first_watch`, checked by `crew.mjs check`).
- **Brand lock.** When a brand applies, the brand step is required (the research lead's check fails without a DESIGN.md), all three looks keep the brand's palette and type and differ only in layout and motion, and `look-payload` refuses an off-brand set.
- **Concept gate before the build.** A Sonnet concept critic (`agents/concept-critic.md`) checks the chosen script and look against the literal brief (product on screen within 3 s, hero moment, tone, end line, brand, understandable on first watch) and writes `story/concept-check.json`; failures are fixed before the motion score.
- **Real product fidelity.** Product films use captured UI, or a faithful HTML/CSS recreation if capture fails, never line-art stand-ins.
- **No ending a turn mid-task.** Hard rule 18: the director never ends its turn while its own background render, crew member or check is still running.
- **Headless console** (`RASANAI_CONSOLE_HEADLESS=1`, recorded in the run's `console.json`). `push`, `ask`, `wait` and the rest work on the run's files with no HTTP server; `wait` applies the effects the server used to apply on each action; session, consumed and rejected files are written atomically under shared locks (`lib/runlock.mjs`). RasanAI Studio 0.6.1 uses it; the browser console is unchanged otherwise.
- Why: a real 30-second ChatGPT for Mac launch test came out as an off-brand museum metaphor with the product on screen only after 19 s, after about 8 hours and $171. Self-test: all checks pass, with new cases for the pick filter, G7, the concept gate, the brand lock and the headless console.
