Type and image

Motifs from the brand and the product

Take recurring shapes and metaphors from this brand and its verbs; pass the logo-swap test.

Takeaways

  • Run the logo-swap test on the whole film.
  • Derive grids and textures from the brand's own vocabulary.
  • Let a physical action stand for a product verb.
  • Pair contrasting materials inside one art direction.

What it means

Swap the logo for a competitor's: if the film still works, the decoration was generic. Motifs that come from the brand's own vocabulary (its grids, dots, type, transparency) or from what the product actually does give identity without stamping the logo everywhere. Contrasting material languages are welcome inside one art direction, such as sharp vectors against organic texture.

In a RasanAI film

Every major motif carries a provenance note: the brand asset or the product verb it comes from. Decoration with no brand or story role is rejected at review. An object transition enacts a product gesture, so the bridge explains something, and a metaphor that obscures the operation is replaced.

Origins and sources

Studio case studies that build identity from the brand's own materials, and a studio framework that translates digital gestures into physical scenarios. The logo-swap test is an editorial check, not a measurement.

Evidence: Studio practice.

  • BUCK, Nothing Playground: dots, grids, pixels, type and transparency
  • Adam Rowe, ManvsMachine for Squarespace: digital gestures made physical
  • Oddfellows, Spotify Premium: the benefit decides the material behaviour
