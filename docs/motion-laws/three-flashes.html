Motion laws/Accessibility

Accessibility

No more than three flashes a second

Never flash full-frame contrast more than three times in any second.

Takeaways

  • Three flashes a second at most.
  • No alternating full-frame black and white.
  • No flashing saturated red.
  • Change an element, not the ground.
  • Screen striped type and grids for regular-pattern hazards too, and offer a reduced-motion version for big zooms and parallax.

What it means

Rapid flashing can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy, so this is a hard limit and not a style choice. Avoid alternating full-frame black and white, avoid flashing saturated red, and change one element on a constant ground instead.

In a RasanAI film

The render gate measures flashes in the finished film (photosensitivity-flash) and fails it above the limit. Flash bursts vary one element on a locked layout and keep the luminance change small.

Origins and sources

WCAG 2.3.1, Three Flashes or Below Threshold (Success Criterion, level A). Ofcom's broadcast guidance sets a similar limit and also covers regular patterns (more than five light and dark stripe pairs, under conditions). WebKit lists zoom, blur, spinning and multi-directional motion as triggers even without flashing. These are conditional tests, not pixel thresholds. Read the source.

Evidence: Studio practice and standards.

  • Ofcom / ITC guidance on flashing images and regular patterns (PDF)
  • WebKit, Responsive Design for Motion
