Story

A clear call to action

End on a verb and a destination, and let it settle long enough to read.

Takeaways

  • A verb plus a destination, in plain words.
  • On the last beat, with the logo.
  • Settled for about 2 to 4 seconds; a URL or a second call to action needs more.
  • A familiar logo-only sting may need less.

What it means

The viewer needs to know the next step and have time to take it in. A bare logo end card gives nothing to do. A verb plus a destination does, if it stays on screen.

In a RasanAI film

The final beat carries a line such as "Download for macOS" or "Join the waitlist" and gives it settled time, 2 to 4 seconds as a starting range to test, while ambient motion keeps the frame alive. See A resolved ending.

Origins and sources

RasanAI launch grammar, studied from real launch films. An engine rule, not an academic law. The final lockup is designed as a shot with readable time. No source supports a universal end-card length; 2 to 4 seconds is a pilot range.

Evidence: Studio practice, and a RasanAI pilot range.
