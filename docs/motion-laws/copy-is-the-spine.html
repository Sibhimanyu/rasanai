Story

Copy is the spine

One thought per line, a few words, one emphasised word.

Takeaways

  • Second person, plain words.
  • Three to seven words on screen at once.
  • Exactly one emphasised word per line.
  • Break lines at grammatical units, never between an article and its noun.
  • Cut words before speeding up the type.

What it means

People read while the picture moves, so the words have to be quick: second person, plain language, three to seven words on screen at once. One word per line carries the turn, and the eye lands on it.

In a RasanAI film

The copy linter flags brochure language and buzzwords. Each line gets exactly one emphasised word (colour, weight, size or italic), and a longer line is split into two beats rather than shown faster.

Origins and sources

Signaling principle, Richard Mayer, Multimedia Learning: cue the one thing that matters. Line breaks follow Netflix's timed-text rules (subtitle rules, borrowed for titles).

Evidence: Learning research, and studio practice.

  • Netflix, Timed Text General Requirements: break at grammatical units
