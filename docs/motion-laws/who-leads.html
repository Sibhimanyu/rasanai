Motion laws/Sound

Sound

Who leads

At every passage, decide whether voice, music or sound is leading, and let the others make room.

Takeaways

  • One leader per passage.
  • Give the music's phrase room after a key line.
  • Assign accents to musical elements, not only the kick.
  • Counterpoint when agreement would be predictable.
  • A sung or tightly interwoven passage may combine them.

What it means

Words and score take turns. After a key spoken line, the music's important phrase gets room before the next line arrives. An accent can follow a swell or a hi-hat rather than the kick. Sometimes sound deliberately leads, or runs in counterpoint to the picture. What fails is everything speaking at once, or music used as wallpaper that forces a rhythm the pictures cannot hold.

In a RasanAI film

The score marks each passage as voiceover-led, music-led or sound-led. Gaps after key lines are compared with the music's phrases, and a truncated flourish is flagged. Each visual accent is tied to a named musical element and its sync error is measured in frames. Counterpoint is allowed when tagged and never against essential instructional cause.

Origins and sources

Editors on duet-style assignment of picture to music, and sound designers on sound that leads or creates friction.

Evidence: Single practitioner, and studio practice.

  • Paddy Bird, Inside the Edit: Duet Theory and musical element assignment
  • Skillbard, Motionographer: sound that subverts the action
  • Stefan Kostaras, pelican sound, Motionographer: anticipation, tension and release
