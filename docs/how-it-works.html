Under the hood

How a sentence becomes a film.

RasanAI Studio is a native Mac app. It doesn't make the film itself. It runs your own Claude Code or OpenAI Codex as the film's director, hands it a crew and an engine, and shows you the work at every step. This page follows one film through all of it, down to the files on your disk.

The big picture

Four layers, and files between them.

Studio, the director and the engine never call each other over a network. Each one reads and writes files in your project folder. That is why you can quit the app mid-film, and why a phone pager like Squeak can answer a call the same way Studio does.

Layer 1RasanAI Studio

The window you use. Every stage is a native SwiftUI screen: no web view, no embedded console. It keeps your film library, launches the director, shows what it is doing, and turns your clicks into answers.

A film's life

Seven steps. Here is what runs under each.

These are the same seven dots you see across Studio's toolbar. Pick one to see what is on your screen, who on the crew is working, and what is written to disk.

Rasan, directorWaiting for an idea

A sentence, a link, or some footage.

You type what you want, paste a product URL, or drop in clips, a logo or a track. Studio creates the film's folder straight away and saves it as a draft. No agent has started yet, so a draft costs nothing.

Your partSay what the film is about. That is the only thing RasanAI ever requires.

UnderneathStudio only

  1. Studio makes ~/Documents/RasanAI/<film>/ with assets/, audio/, compositions/ and exports/.
  2. Files you drop in are copied in as managed copies. Your originals are never moved or changed.
  3. The film appears in the sidebar under Recent, with its status.

Rasan, directorGetting ready to roll

The essentials, then the director starts.

Studio suggests a film type, a length and a shape from your sentence. You can also answer a few taste questions, pick a brand, and set the pace: Fast, Standard or Thorough. Fast is the default: about two minutes of research, one agent per scene, and one review round.

Your partAdjust anything, then press Start film.

Underneaththe director is launched

  1. Studio starts your CLI headless in the film's folder: claude --print --output-format stream-json, or codex exec --json with a workspace-write sandbox.
  2. A scoped permission list goes with it. The director may read the engine, write inside the project and run node, npx hyperframes and ffmpeg. It may not use sudo, delete recursively, run installers, or git push.
  3. director-driver.mjs supervises the process. Pause and Resume really stop and continue it, and it stops a director that repeats the same tool call five times in a row.
  4. The director's first instruction: read SKILL.md. Never start a server.

Tara, scoutResearching the product

Research first, then one sentence.

Before a single word of script, a research crew reads the product's site, help center, changelog and brand pages and collects its real screens. With your permission it also reads the product's code on your Mac. Their notes merge into one truth sheet, and every fact on screen has to trace back to it.

Your call 1 of 5Read the brief sentence and what the research found. Fix a word, or carry on.

Underneathcrew in parallel

  1. The director plans the crew with crew.mjs plan and writes each member a brief adapted to the model that will run it.
  2. Product, brand, screens and precedent researchers run at once. A research lead merges them into the truth sheet.
  3. console.mjs push writes the Brief into session.json. Studio sees the file change and shows the call as a native screen.
  4. Your answer becomes one line in actions.jsonl. console.mjs wait picks it up and marks it in consumed.json.

Kavi, writerWriting three scripts

Three scripts: Sure, Bold and Wild.

Three writers each pitch one film from the truth sheet, picking from 70 narrative devices. A script editor reads them like a hostile client. Each script is a title, a logline and every timed beat: what is on screen, what is said and what we see. There is no look yet, so you judge the story as words and timing.

Your call 2 of 5Pick one, ask for three more, or tell Claude what to change.

Underneathwriters' room

  1. Three script writers run in parallel. A concept critic throws out any pitch that would work for any product.
  2. A moves pass gives each script the one object that travels through the film, and bans the obvious ideas.
  3. story.mjs check scores each script against the story rubric before you see it.
story/crew/decisions.json
Chitra, art directorDesigning three looks

Three design systems, made for this story.

There is no style menu. A design researcher studies the story's subject, then three designers each blend two to four references from a library of 108 design systems and 64 motion styles into a new system. Each is a complete DESIGN.md, drawn on your film's own first line. If you picked a brand, the first look is your brand, extended for motion.

Your call 3 of 5Pick a look, ask for new blends near one, or turn the energy up or down.

Underneaththe design desk

  1. A design researcher, then three design-system designers in parallel.
  2. Each system's specimen is rendered and measured. A gate fails off-palette colour, poor contrast, fonts that don't exist, and generic defaults. It also fails any two systems that are too alike.
  3. The chosen system becomes the film's DESIGN.md, and its motion language becomes motion.md, a contract of eases, durations and holds.

Laya, motion directorScoring the motion

The whole film, rough, at its real length.

A motion director scores every shot in 2D or real 3D, and every cut with exact handoff numbers on both sides. The music is edited to picture on bar lines, so it ends when the film ends. The preview puts each scene's key frame at its real duration, with the music and the voice under it.

Your call 4 of 5Play it, and click any moment to leave a note. Then the scenes are built.

Underneathscore, then build

  1. The motion director writes the score. music.mjs finds beats, bars, sections and a real ending using Node and FFmpeg only.
  2. Your notes come back as comment actions pinned to a scene, a time and a point on the frame.
  3. A short film (45 seconds or less) gets one film builder. A longer one gets one animator per scene, all at once. Each writes HyperFrames HTML and checks its own motion frame by frame.
motion/score.jsonmusic/animatic.jsoncompositions/
Rasika, criticChecking every frame

Checked, finished, rendered.

Before you see the film, it has to pass the gates, and critics who never saw it being made judge it fresh. Anything that fails goes back to the scene's animator, at most twice. Then the finish adds real motion blur and one grade across every scene, and the film is rendered.

Your call 5 of 5Note a moment, apply the notes, then export.

Underneathgates, finish, export

  1. The ship gates run: story.mjs, slop.mjs, obey.mjs, motion-gate.mjs, stage3d.mjs check and sound.mjs check.
  2. finish.mjs renders sub-frames for motion blur (up to 32 samples), then adds grain, halation, a vignette and an optional LUT.
  3. Export the original, an MP4 at up to 1080p or 720p, SRT or VTT captions, or captions burned in. Every version is kept, with what changed between them.

How the app and the director talk

One file to read. One file to append to.

The director's state lives in session.json. Only the engine writes it, atomically, by writing a temporary file and renaming it. Studio only reads it. Answers go the other way, as one JSON line appended to actions.jsonl under a lock file.

  • Nothing is lost. An answer that hasn't been picked up yet is pending, not missing. Studio shows it as sent and never writes it twice.
  • Bad input stops at the door. A line with an unknown step, a bad id or over 1 MB is listed in rejected.json with the reason, and never reaches the director.
  • It is a published contract. The protocol is versioned ("schema": 1), so other apps can answer too. That is how Squeak lets you make a call from your phone.

The crew, for real

Each character is a group of agents.

The faces in Studio stand for real jobs. Behind them are 35 role briefs. Each one is a separate agent with its own instructions, the files it must produce, and a check its work must pass before the director accepts it. The crew never talks to you directly.

The checks

Nothing ships on a hope.

Each gate is a script that reads the actual work, not the agent's description of it. A check that couldn't run is never reported as a pass.

Story

story.mjs check rejects the default arc, and any pitch that would work for any product.

Anti-slop

slop.mjs catches the tells in copy, look, motion, timing and sound, from "seamless" to glowing text to a whoosh on every cut.

Motion contract

obey.mjs opens every scene in headless Chrome and checks every GSAP tween against motion.md.

Fluency

motion-gate.mjs measures the draft frame by frame: motion in at least 75% of frames, no freeze over 0.8 s, and text that fits.

3D and sound

stage3d.mjs check: the same pixels at the same time, and never blank. sound.mjs check: −14 LUFS, no audible loop, no dead air.

Rendering

Scenes are web pages. Frames are exact.

Every scene is an HTML composition with one paused GSAP timeline. The renderer doesn't play the page; it seeks it to each frame's exact time and takes a picture. So the same time always gives the same pixels, on every render.

  1. 1 · ComposeHyperFrames HTMLOne composition per scene, in the film's DESIGN.md, timed by data- attributes.
  2. 2 · AnimateGSAP, three.js2D on GSAP 3.14. 3D on Rasan3D, a three.js r181 runtime clocked by the same timeline.
  3. 3 · CaptureHeadless ChromeEach frame is seeked and captured. The finish renders sub-frames and averages them for real motion blur.
  4. 4 · GradeOne look, every cutGrain, halation, vignette and an optional LUT, the same on every scene, so cuts never change texture.
  5. 5 · DeliverFFmpegEncodes the MP4 and mixes the edited music and effects to −14 LUFS, plus a poster and captions.
  • Real 3D, not a demo. Rasan3D averages several renders per frame for motion blur, depth of field and anti-aliasing. Simulations are a pure function of time, so a seek always lands on the same frame.
  • Every word on its note. For lyric videos, RasanMusic lights each word on its sung start, read as a function of time.
  • The cost, stated. Motion blur costs about 3× a normal render at 8 samples and 10 to 12× at 32. Drafts skip it. 3D frames with depth of field can take seconds each without a GPU.
  • Stepped looks stay sharp. A scene animated on twos or threes can opt out of the blur, so its steps stay hard.

On your disk

A film is a folder you can open.

Studio's library is ~/Documents/RasanAI. Search, rename, archive and duplicate films in the app, or open the folder in Finder. Nothing is kept inside the app.

Every decision has a reason

decisions.json is what the Decisions drawer (⌥⌘D) shows: the story device, the look, the motion, the transitions and the music edit. Each one names the obvious option it passed over.

The look travels

The chosen system is a complete DESIGN.md: colours by role, real fonts, motion and camera. Any agent or design tool can build from it. Brands live in Brands/ and are reused across films.

Pick up where you left off

The run folder holds the whole state. Quit mid-film and the director resumes from it; there is no session to lose.

What leaves your Mac

Local by design. Honest about the rest.

Studio has no account, no server and no open port. But making a film uses outside services, so here is where your data goes.

Stays on your Mac

Your film folders, your footage, the engine and its scripts, music analysis, every gate, and the render itself.

Goes to your AI provider

Your director sends prompts and project context to Anthropic or OpenAI, under your own account and their terms. Check your agent's permissions before sharing private material.

May use the web

Research reads public pages. Site capture, voice, music and fonts can use HyperFrames, Google Fonts or Fontshare. Updates are fetched from GitHub.

Tested

Installing the app

First launch, step by step.

The beta is ad-hoc signed but not yet notarized by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm the first time. Managed Macs may not allow it.

Download for Mac

Release notes · SHA-256 checksum · Update feed · Source and build instructions

  1. Drag RasanAI Studio into Applications before you open it, so updates can replace it.
  2. Right-click the app and choose Open, or use Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Install and sign in to Claude Code or Codex if you haven't already. Studio finds it and shows its sign-in status in Settings (⌘,).
  4. Choose Help → Prepare Rendering Tools. It installs FFmpeg and HyperFrames into ~/Library/Application Support/RasanAI/Tools. Node is already inside the app.
  5. Describe your first film. After that, signed updates arrive on their own.

Prefer the terminal?

The same engine, as a skill.

The engine inside Studio is the RasanAI skill for Claude Code. Install it on its own and type /rasanai. Your calls then open in a Director's Console in your browser, on 127.0.0.1 with a per-session token. It needs Node 20 or newer (it fetches a private copy if you have none), the HyperFrames skills, and FFmpeg.
