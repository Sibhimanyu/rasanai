# Reading a style reference: take the rule, not the frames

A reference film for a LOOK is not a beat sheet. Copying its parts on its own order of scenes gives clip-art and a film that cannot say anything about the new subject. What transfers is the rule it obeys. (From onetake's reference reading, PolyForm Noncommercial: the idea only, in our words.)

1. **The one rule every object obeys.** Find the single constraint that makes every object in the reference belong: every word sits on a machined part (pills with two rivets, plates with four screws, a cursor with a hole in its tail); every shape is a circle, a half-circle or a quarter. Palette, grid and type follow from it. The rule applied to the NEW subject reads as the style; copied parts without the rule read as clip-art.
2. **The tool behind the look.** Ask what software or technique made the reference (offset bands, duplicator echoes, per-glyph typing, a plotter pen). The look is full of what was cheap there. Knowing it separates the signature from what was merely easy; most of it is a few lines of `f(t)` here.
3. **Numbers, not impressions.** Take palette hex values from full frames, the grid pitch from one pixel row, the type size from cap height at 1080p, a part's radius as a share of the part. They go at the top of the composition as constants, and are marked as measured in the DESIGN.md.
4. **Its objects, our carries.** If the reference hard-cuts between beats, that cut is not part of its style. Carry every boundary with the style's OWN parts (the track's edges stretch into rails, the pill becomes the reel's window, the camera goes down a bolt hole). See the carry rules in `references/moves.md`.
5. **Its language, our meaning.** Make the rule say something about the new subject, and let the beats come from that (the story devices), not from the reference's order of scenes.

Use: when the brief or the user gives a reference film for a LOOK, the design desk writes a half-page "rule card" (the rule, the tool, the numbers, the objects) before any look is drafted, and the first draft is checked against it, not against the reference's frames. A frame of the reference never goes into the film.
