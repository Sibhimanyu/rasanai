# The design desk: bespoke design systems, per film

RasanAI does not show a menu of styles and does not pick one from a list. For every film a **design desk** researches the subject on the spot and builds **three new design systems** for the chosen story by blending references from an internal library. The user sees exactly those three at the Look (drawn live on their own first line) and picks one. This file is the desk's reference: who does what, the file formats, the library and the gate.

## The flow

1. **Design researcher** (`agents/design-researcher.md`), during the Brief, in parallel with the research desk: the subject's own visual world, its category's clichés, its audience, its materials, places and eras, with sources, plus a shortlist of 8 to 12 library references with why. Writes `research/design.md` and `research/design-refs.json`.
2. **Three design-system designers** (`agents/design-system-designer.md`, keys Sure, Bold, Wild), after the story is chosen: each builds ONE system for that story by blending library references (2 to 4; a single-medium look may cite fewer, see `blend.json`) with the subject's world, and pulls **1 to 3 style references** from `library/styles/` when they fit the brief (see the library). Writes `design/<label>/{DESIGN.md, specimen.html, recipe.json, blend.json, grammar.json}`. With a brand DESIGN.md, **Sure is the brand extended for motion** (its colours and type exactly, plus motion contract, camera and components); Bold and Wild push around it.
3. **The gate**: `design.mjs check-system --dir design/<label>` per system (exit 0 or 2; it also needs a valid `grammar.json`), `design.mjs check-systems --run "$RUN"` for the three together (legacy looks: three different grammars; a medium look is held to differing in the five axes instead, and may share a grammar id).
4. **The Look**: `design.mjs look-payload --run "$RUN" --hook "<first line>"` is the console payload: exactly three styles, each with its name, the blend in one line, why it is right for this story, and the recipe the console draws live (each system's own `specimen.html` in a sandboxed iframe, `specimen.png` as the poster; `recipe.json` through `RasaPresets` / `RasaPresets3D` only when a system has no specimen). `more` asks the desk for new blends near the selected one; there is no style browser.
5. **`choose`**: `design.mjs choose-system --run "$RUN" --label <Sure|Bold|Wild> --decisions "$RUN/decisions.json"` makes the chosen DESIGN.md the film's look: `look/DESIGN.md`, `look/frame.md` (through the same DESIGN.md to frame.md path as a brand), the system's taxonomy picks in decisions, then `motion-md.mjs write --design-system` writes the binding motion.md from the system's motion contract and `direction.mjs compile` writes DIRECTION.md with the system's own words (blend, subject world, Motion and camera) in it.
6. **Lyric videos**: the treatment writers each carry a style bible; after the user picks a treatment, one designer (key = that treatment's label) builds the bible out into the complete system with the same gate. There is no Look picker.

## Custom styles are not Look candidates

A user's saved custom style (`references/custom-styles.md`) is an explicit asset the owner chose to reuse. It never replaces, pre-fills or ranks among the three bespoke Look candidates the desk makes for a film, and the desk never writes into the user's Styles library. A style can be extracted from a finished run (`custom-style.mjs create`), and only then enters later films on request.

## The brand lock

When the brief names a brand (`brand_name` / `use_brand`) or the product is a known brand, the brand step always runs first (the brand researcher's `research/brand/DESIGN.md`, or the workspace DESIGN.md, in decisions as `brand`). Then **all three looks keep the brand's colour, type and logo locked**: its palette (canvas, ink, accent), its type system, its UI language and logo usage. What varies is the **style world** (pixel, collage, 3D, editorial, illustration, a world made of images), the composition, the layout, the motion language and the density. The gates enforce it: `design.mjs check-system` fails a system whose canvas, ink, accent or display face is not the brand's (the label does not matter), `check-systems` then requires the three to differ in style world, layout and motion instead of palette and display face, and `look-payload` refuses to push (or recommend) an off-brand look. Product truth (`references/product-first.md`) still holds: the real product and its claim stay unmistakable in every look. An invented world is allowed when it carries that.

### A branded launch, promo or brand film: three looks, the brand locked

A branded launch gets **three looks too**. **Locked:** the brand's colour (canvas, ink, accent), type system, logo and UI language, and any declared motion limit. **Varies:** the style world (pixel, collage, 3D, editorial, illustration, image worlds), the medium, the layout and the motion language.

- **Look A (Sure)** = the brand film's own grammar exactly (`brand-film/FILM-STYLE.md`, `references/brand-film.md`).
- **Looks B and C (Bold, Wild)** = the brand's colour, type and logo in a different style world, chosen from `library/styles/`. The desk **may draw on movements, studios, materials, style presets and the style atlas** for B and C: pick a trait or two, keep at least one signature feature, and let the brand supply the colour, type and logo. The design researcher returns a shortlist for these.
- `blend.json` carries the references used (`references: [...]`), `film_style: "<path to FILM-STYLE.md>"` and `film_style_takes: [3 or more things taken from the card]`; for B and C also `style_world: "<id>"` and what stays locked.
- The strict form (the look is the card exactly, no outside reference at all) applies only when the user asks for a strict brand film (`strict_brand_film` in decisions.json, never inferred).
- `design.mjs check-system` / `check-systems` / `look-payload` refuse a look with a canvas, ink or accent that is not a colour of the card (dE over 15, accent over 28) or a display face not in the card's typefaces; a library reference is **not** a refusal on B and C. Finish, depth, camera and 3D are never refused on a flat card (`references/brand-film.md` section 4).

## The library (raw material for every film; branded films use it for looks B and C)

`library/systems/**` (movements, studios, vernacular, material: palette logic, type, grid, shape, motion and camera, how to instruct a model to build it, cheap tells, sources) and `library/motion/**` (2D and 3D motion and camera styles: timings, eases, lens, recipes for HyperFrames GSAP and Rasan3D, pitfalls). `scripts/library.mjs index` merges the index fragments and every entry's frontmatter into `library/index.json` (rerunnable); `search --q "<words>" [--space 3d] [--kind <kind>]` ranks entries; `show <id> [--full]` reads one. **Style references** (`library/styles/<slug>.md`, 30 of them, `library/styles/README.md` is the table): whole looks imported from four MIT repositories (credit in `third_party/lemo-opuscar/`, `third_party/mg-styles-15/`, `third_party/animate/` and `third_party/gbro/`): the launch and product ones from lemo-opuscar (glass-product, dark-keynote, dataviz, blueprint, halftone-dossier, art-deco, ascii-crt, swiss-motion, living-screencast, hologram-hud, iso-infographic) and the 15 motion-graphics idioms of mg-styles-15 (flat-vector, line-art, isometric, 3d-render, cel-boil, collage, liquid, morph, bauhaus, synthwave, aurora-glass, hanazi, paperclip, pixel, hud) and three print and drawing looks from animate (riso, sketchbook, manim-math) and halftone-collage from gbro. Each has essence and what it is NOT, materials, colour logic, type, motion, camera grammar, 3 to 6 signature features, when to use, and a source line. **When to pull one:** when the brief or the subject's world already points at it (a hardware launch with an invisible mechanism: glass-product; an interface-led software launch: dark-keynote; a claim proved by a number: dataviz; a how-it-works teardown: blueprint, hologram-hud; a system or supply chain: iso-infographic; a rigorous brand: swiss-motion, bauhaus; a calm AI-product promise: aurora-glass; a walkthrough with a character: living-screencast). Pull one to three, read them in full, take one or two traits from each, and say in `blend.json` which (`style_refs`). They are references to blend from, never presets: a system that is one style with a new name fails the desk's own review, and a style is not pulled just because it is on the list (a film about a ledger does not become a hologram). The same shape (essence and what it is not, materials, colour logic, type, motion, camera grammar as a move/what it expresses/what it serves table) is the model for a system's own `## Motion and camera` and `## Overview`. Like the rest of the library they are for unbranded films only.

The 403 style presets in `taxonomy/presets/` stay as extra raw material the designers may cite; they are not shown to the user and not picked from.

Blend, don't copy: take one or two traits from each of two to four references (a palette logic, a grid, a texture, a timing curve, a camera grammar), add the subject's own world, and the result must be something no single reference is. A blend that reads as one reference with a new name fails the desk's own review (a single-medium look passes by saying what is its own).

## File formats

### `design/<label>/DESIGN.md`

The format `scripts/lib/design-md.mjs` reads (`references/brand.md`). Frontmatter: `name`, `description`, `colors:` by role with `#` comments (`canvas`, `ink`, `accent`, `support`, `surface`, `muted`, `rule`), `typography:` (`display`, `body`, `mono`: `fontFamily`, `fontWeight`), `rounded:`, `shadows:`, `components:` (at least two). Body: `## Overview`, `## Colors`, `## Typography`, `## Layout and shape`, `## Components`, `## Motion and camera`, `## Do's and Don'ts`, `## Signature features` (always) and `## Imagery` when the film has generated images. A complete, gate-passing example is in `references/design-desk-example/Sure/`.

**`## Motion and camera`** is what the animators build from, so it is numbers: the duration scale in ms, the holds (reading time only, each naming what keeps moving while it reads: for a legacy look the motion gate fails any still stretch of 0.8 s and any camera slower than 4% a second; a medium contract may declare intentional holds for named scenes), the stagger, the eases by GSAP name (enter, exit, move), what is banned, the signature entrance; the camera for flat scenes (locked, a push, parallax layers, a rack of focus); and, when the story has depth, a 3D camera grammar from the library's motion entries (lens in mm, camera height, moves and their easing, light, depth of field).

**`## Signature features`** is the system's contract: 3 to 6 concrete, checkable traits that make the look this look and nothing else, each one a sentence a juror can answer yes or no to from a frame or a clip ("every edge has a 2 px ink line", "grain on every plate", "the accent appears on exactly one element per frame", "every move lands on the 8 px grid", "no gradient anywhere"). Numbers and nouns, not adjectives; no trait about the story or the copy. The frame juror (`agents/frame-juror.md`) and the critic (`agents/critic.md`) check each by name on the finished film and report which hold and which fail, so a system that cannot keep one of its own features must change the feature, not be forgiven. A trait a style reference names (below) may be taken whole or adapted; a system that cites a style reference keeps at least one of that style's signature features.

**`## Imagery`** (a presenter film requires it; elsewhere it is optional) is the art direction every generated image obeys, 25 words at least: the medium, the lens, the light and its direction, how the system's colours map into a picture, the texture, where the composition leaves room (for type or a person), and what never appears. Image prompts (`references/imagery.md`) and `presenter.mjs plates` condense it; `design.mjs check-system` fails a presenter run's system without it.

**Meaning-keyed colour fields** (unbranded films; a brand's own palette always wins): when a film uses a flat colour field per beat or chapter (a paper-collage, poster or title-card look), the field is chosen by what the idea MEANS, and a system's `## Colors` says so in a table of meaning to colour: burnt orange or red for labour, time spent and urgency; mustard for a tool, a warning and experience leaking away; deep green for cognition, taste and a system reset; deep purple for norms, sediment and long memory; teal for judgement, collaboration and automatic execution. The designer picks its own hues, keeps one design language (same treatment, shadow and type) across the different fields, and lets a chapter hold ONE field, so colour changes mean the idea changed. A field chosen because it is pretty, or one default (cobalt blue everywhere), is a finding for the frame juror: ask what the colour says. Library reference: `library/styles/halftone-collage.md`.

### `design/<label>/grammar.json`

The look's ONE motion grammar (`library/grammars/<id>.json`, schema in `scripts/grammar.mjs check`; a bespoke grammar uses the same schema). **Legacy schema** (no `grammar_format`): the **frame device** and how it moves, the **type** classes and behaviour (always readable, never cropped at rest), the **image** behaviour, 8 to 12 **techniques** (id, what, a concrete build route with GSAP, CSS, SVG or clip-path, or Rasan3D, and timings), the **signature** move, the **density** (`change_every_s`), the palette (which a brand overrides), do and don't, `fits` and `flat_ok`. The system answers "what does it look like", the grammar answers "how does the frame move". Six are seeded: `editorial-grid`, `glyph-chain`, `viewfinder-reel`, `specimen-sheet`, `glow-panels`, `dot-system`. The three looks use three different grammars; a grammar with `flat_ok: false` (its look is glow or blur on every element) is never given to a graphic-route brand (the brand film measured no finish: `finish.layerOff`); hero effects are budgets in every grammar. `grammar.mjs write --run "$RUN" --id <id> --label <label>` copies it in, merged with the brand palette when a brand DESIGN.md exists. The Motion Director later names a technique and the frame device's move for every beat (`score.grammar`), and the film builder inlines those techniques.

**`medium-grammar@2`** (`grammar_format`; a look that carries `medium.json` or `medium_policy_revision`): the policies the look chose, not a quota. Declare `text_role` (`none` | `proof` | `support` | `primary` | `mixed`; `none` is valid, no phantom text), `frame_device.what`, 1 or more `native_actions` (`id`, `name`, `what`, `impl_ref` = a registered id or an existing path in the skill), optional `layers`, `camera`, `transitions` (an empty list is valid), `ground_motion` as prose or `{mode: "none", reason}`, and `density.mode` (`reading-led` | `action-led` | `music-led` | `manual` | `chosen` with a reason; `change_every_s` optional). `word-choreo` is required only when `uses_word_choreo` is true. Each score beat names a declared action. The medium contract (`medium.json`, `references/medium-direction.md`) says which craft checks it qualifies per layer and scene; hard checks (proof, legibility, safe area, brand, rights) are never qualified.

### `design/<label>/specimen.html`

The system drawn as a key frame on the chosen story's first on-screen line (`story/chosen.json` beats[0].on_screen), by the same bar as `agents/frame-designer.md`: a standalone 1600 x 900 page (`data-width` / `data-height` on the root), the system's own components and layout (the example: a receipt on ledger paper, mono figures, a vermilion stamp landing on the total), real fonts, the accent rationed as the DESIGN.md says, the headline as DOM text. GSAP is loaded from the CDN tag (the console and the gate substitute the vendored copy). It is a **moving sketch of the chosen story's open and first rung in the look's grammar** (the frame device moving, the first line in its type class, one or two techniques), a paused GSAP timeline at `window.__specimen = { tl }`, 4 to 5 s, every tween with an ease named in the Motion and camera section, finished with `tl.progress(1)` so the page rests on its final frame; the console plays it from 0 on hover. A 3D-leaning system may use Rasan3D (`stage3d.mjs install --dest design/<label>`). `blend.json` may add `"specimen": {"accent_max": 0.3, "canvas": "absent"}` to override the gate's defaults. `design.mjs check-system` renders it to `specimen.png` (the console's poster), and `crew.mjs strip --file design/<label>/specimen.html --from 0 --to 5 --fps 6 --out ...` shows the hover motion. The worked example is `references/design-desk-example/Sure/specimen.html`.

### `design/<label>/recipe.json`

The fallback specimen (used only if a system has no `specimen.html`, and still required: it is what `RasaPresets` draws).

```json
{ "name": "Counting House", "tagline": "ledger paper, banknote green, one vermilion stamp",
  "recipe": { "palette": {"canvas","ink","accent","accent2","surface","muted"}, "fonts": {"display","body","mono","displayWeight","bodyWeight"},
              "layout": "record", "radius": 2, "stroke": {"width": 2, "style": "solid"}, "shadow": "soft", "surface": "paper",
              "texture": "paper", "icons": "line", "motif": "rules", "motion": "snap", "effects": [] },
  "three": { "base": "glass-3d", "note": "only for a 3D-leaning system" } }
```

The recipe is exactly what `RasaPresets.render()` takes (`scripts/lib/vocab.mjs` lists every allowed value). Its palette and fonts are the DESIGN.md's. `three.base` names one of the 3D specimen families `RasaPresets3D` can draw (the system's palette and fonts are applied to it), so a 3D-leaning system shows real depth at the Look.

### `design/<label>/blend.json`

`name`, `one_line` (the blend, in one line the user reads), `why_for_story` (one line, for this story), `style_refs[]` (optional: 1 to 3 slugs from `library/styles/`, each with the traits taken in `references` style, `{id, traits[], why}`), `references[{id, traits[], why}]` (2 to 4 real library ids; a single-medium look may cite 0 or 1 when `originality` (20+ characters: what makes it this film's own) or `style_refs` says what it is made from, and never invents a hybrid to reach a count; on a strict branded film `[]`, with `film_style` and `film_style_takes` instead), `subject_world[]`, `stack` (taxonomy ids: `visual-style`, `motion-language`, so DIRECTION.md compiles) and `motion_contract` (`language`, `scale_ms` (4 or more), `easing {enter, exit, move}`, `stagger_ms`, `hold_ms`, `banned`): the binding numbers `motion.md` and `obey.mjs` use, which must match the Motion and camera section.

## The gate (`design.mjs check-system`, `lib/system.mjs`)

Fails (exit 2) on: files missing (including `specimen.html`); a specimen that errors in the browser, whose rendered PNG is more than 50% off the system's palette, has no canvas/surface or no ink or no accent on it, or whose accent covers more than the DESIGN.md allows (8% when it says the accent is rationed or never fills a background, else 15%), whose first line is not in the DOM, whose timeline is missing, over 2.5 s, or uses eases the Motion and camera section doesn't name; DESIGN.md that doesn't parse with canvas, ink, accent and a display font, radii, components, at least 3 do's and 3 don'ts, an overview; ink on canvas under 4.5:1; a recipe that doesn't draw, or that disagrees with the DESIGN.md's palette and fonts; a font that is not on Google Fonts, Fontshare or in the project's files; a generic default (Inter or system type for everything, the purple-blue gradient, the "Claude look" of cream, rust and an italic serif, unless the brand is that); a blend citing fewer than two library ids that exist (or a reference without traits or a why); no `subject_world`; stack ids that don't resolve; a Motion and camera section without timings, eases and a camera (and, for a `three` system, a lens in mm); a motion contract that is incomplete. `check-systems` also fails when two of the three are alike (they must differ in at least three of palette, display face (always), layout, motion language) or blend exactly the same references, or when their specimens are the same layout recoloured (luminance and edge correlation above 0.85). `--brand <DESIGN.md>` makes Sure's colours and display face the brand's. For a presenter film: a missing or thin (under 25 words) `## Imagery` section.

Warnings (not blocking): an accent under 3:1 on the canvas, a defaulty display face, a thin motion section, a font that couldn't be verified offline.

## Comparing looks, the visual bible and continuity (Plan 04)

- **Comparison.** `design.mjs compare-plan --run $RUN` picks ONE approved story beat (real copy, evidence, product imagery) and writes `design/comparison-plan.json`; every look is drawn on that same beat, one styleframe per look, a justified second only for a real uncertainty. Three options stay where the user expects three; a supplied brand keeps its logo, factual UI and approved colours and the looks vary hierarchy, density, material, framing and motion character inside it. A saved series' look is locked and carries its provenance. No shot list, board or production scene exists before the Look is committed.
- **Craft class.** `blend.json` may name `look_style` (`dimensional`, `flat-graphic`, `editorial`, `photographic`, `character`, or another word). Depth of field, tilt, a lit ground, a different shot size every frame and 3-6x UI are the **dimensional product** craft: opt-in, enforced by the frame checks only for that class (`lib/frames-lib.mjs` `craftProfile`); for the others they are review evidence, never failures. Structure (shot ids, essential text, timings, refs, protected proof text) is checked for every look. A look is drawn on the same approved beat as the other options: distinct in composition, visual protagonist, spatial logic and material or motion behaviour, never a recolour.
- **How looks differ.** `blend.axes` (hierarchy, density, material, framing, motion_character); `check-systems` fails `look-axes-repeat` when two looks differ on fewer than 3. The card (`look-payload` `styles[].card`) states the 2 to 3 consequential differences, what stays fixed and any limitation (a missing font, no real screen) before the commit.
- **Visual bible.** `choose-system` writes `look/visual-bible.json`: palette by role, exact font identities with locale fallback, safe regions, material/light/camera conventions, motion vocabulary (from the motion contract), transitions by semantic relation (continuation, contrast, example, cause, scale change, separation), imagery and finishing treatments each with what they serve, variation (constant vs varies, at most six film-wide commitments), continuity anchors, `constraints.hard` (brand) apart from `constraints.defaults` (creative) and local, approved exceptions. It carries a source digest; `bible-check` says when it is stale; `bible-for --scene` gives a worker its subset. DESIGN.md and the motion contract stay the authored sources.
- **References.** For each reference used: the principle, the evidence, the transfer to this brand, what not to copy, where it does not apply; a small set, units normalized (60 fps frame counts to seconds or an explicit frame lock; pixels as a fraction of their reference dimensions). Inspiration links and original analysis stay apart from any bundled asset.
- **Revision.** "Same typography, less glossy" is a bounded look revision (`design.mjs look-revise`): Story kept, the fixed parts listed, only frames and build outputs drawn from a changed part go stale (`lib/impact.mjs`).
- **Continuity.** `look/continuity.json`: shared components each with one owner, and seam types. See `scripts/lib/continuity.mjs` (`design.mjs continuity-check`).
On an **Evidence film** only (the Brief picked it), the technique guides in `library/evidence/` (satellite briefing, engineering board, LiDAR survey, white model, data physicalization, exploded teardown, microscope, archive light table) tell the desk which real data a grammar can carry: `references/evidence.md`. They are not a preset menu and are never offered on another film.
