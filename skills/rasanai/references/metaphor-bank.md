# Metaphor bank for explainers (abstract words to images)

FOR EXPLAINERS ONLY (faceless explainers, concept films, narrated scripts with abstract nouns). A product-first launch film shows the real product and never swaps it for a metaphor (`references/product-first.md`); do not use this bank there.

Ask what the word looks like, or what it is the opposite of. Use each metaphor ONCE per film; reusing a visual reads as padding. Take props from the audience's own culture (a snack pouch, a group chat, a muddy running shoe at dawn), not the category's clip-art (rockets for launch, shields for security, lightbulbs for ideas).

| The script says | What worked |
|---|---|
| "the top 1%" | 100 identical dots or people, everything dims except one, which takes the accent; or a pie whose 1% slice the camera pushes into |
| "8 principles" | 8 abstract shapes that gather into one form: the number and the concept become one image |
| "getting attention" | a light sweep across a dark field of identical things, catching one |
| "over a weekend" | a calendar whose last two days light up |
| "commoditised" | many identical objects flooding in; "differentiated" is one thing standing apart |
| "a blank canvas" | an actual empty artboard with a cursor about to draw |
| "billion-dollar" | the word set at 3 to 4x the surrounding type, alone in frame |
| "stop scrolling" | cut on the action: a feed that halts dead on the word |
| the product "thinks" or "works" | an object from the customer's world (a wok, a ledger, the real log), never an orb or a sparkle |

## Consequence over claim

Show the consequence, not the claim written large: a line crosses a threshold and a notification fires; the bill drops and the next row slides up. A viewer believes what they watch happen, and a counted or watched quantity (a hundred dots with one lit) is believed where a bigger number is not.

## Script cues

Mark each narration line with its cue before designing: numbers get a visual built from the number (never a caption); abstract nouns get a metaphor from this bank; names get a name or logo beat with its own hold; contrast words ("but", "not anymore", "instead") get a turn (a change of ground, colour, scale or camera). Not every word goes on screen: show the one or two that matter. Mute test: with the sound off, does the picture alone tell the same story?
