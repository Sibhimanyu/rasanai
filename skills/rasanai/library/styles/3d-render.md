# 3D render (C4D/Blender finish)

Soft, satisfying CGI: candy materials, cloner-array ripples, physics-feel jiggle and a held hero composition.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

A solid-feeling pastel product ident in the Cinema 4D / Octane tradition: soft gummy, glossy plastic and frosted glass or chrome accent materials on an infinite cyc under big area lights, with a MoGraph-style cloner field rippling under an effector and a hero object dropping in with damped-spring jiggle.

Not: flat 2D shapes, neon synthwave, or glass-in-black-studio dispersion renders.

## Materials

- PBR materials: subsurface-scattered gummy, glossy plastic, frosted glass or chrome; ambient occlusion, soft reflections, three-point HDRI lighting on an infinite cyc.
- Shallow depth of field (f/1.8 feel), real motion blur, 24 fps.

## Colour logic

- Pastel candy colours (pink, peach, cream, lilac) with one chrome or glass accent material.

## Type

- Inflated or bevelled 3D wordmark as the hero; supporting type minimal.

## Motion

- Cloner arrays with random or plain effectors produce wave-like group motion; the hero collides with and displaces the field.
- Long-tail ease (80 percent of a move in the first 20 percent) with a very slow finish; hits are frame-synced to impacts.

## Camera grammar

- Long-tail eased moves ending on a clean hero composition held about a second.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Believable soft materials (subsurface gummy, glossy plastic) lit by soft area lights.
2. A cloner field of repeated pills or spheres animated by an effector wave.
3. A hero object with damped-spring squash-and-stretch collision.
4. Shallow depth of field and real motion blur at 24 fps.
5. A long-tail eased camera landing on a held hero composition.

## When to use

- Brand idents, consumer products, packaging and app icons rendered as objects; playful premium launches that need tactile satisfaction.

Avoid when: Flat brand-locked UI systems, tight render budgets, data-dense stories.
