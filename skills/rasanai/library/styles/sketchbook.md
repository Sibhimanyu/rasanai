# Sketchbook

Graphite and one accent colour on a warm sketchbook page: pencilled in, inked over, coloured with a scribble, hand lettering as the narrator, the page as the world.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

The picture is a page in a sketchbook and the whole film stays on it. The camera moves across the same page (a dot grid, a spiral binding, smudges, a coffee ring, the edges of the pages below) instead of cutting away. Things are drawn on in order: pencil first, ink once it is decided, then a quick colour scribble. Hand lettering does the narrating. Structure is fixed (where a scribble goes, how long a hatch runs); only the tremor changes, every 2 frames, so everything boils and nothing swims.

Not: a doodle font over a clean vector scene, a whiteboard marker explainer, a childlike crayon drawing, or a sketch filter on a photo. Not matched: the demo character and the kit's props.

## Materials

- Paper `#f2ead8`, faint tooth specks, fibres, a soft vignette, a dot grid (or ruled lines), graphite smudges, ghosts of erased lines, one coffee ring, a spiral binding along the top. Built once from a seeded generator and cached; a faint grain boils on top.
- Two pens: ink `#2a2420` is one confident stroke plus a thin second pass that does not quite agree; graphite `#6f6a63` (light `#9a948b`) is two light, looser passes, for anything provisional.
- Colour sits a few pixels OFF the line, the way a quick colour pass misses the outline. Shading is hatching (or a scribble over a faint wash), never a gradient; a round thing is hatched with a lit circle left open top-left.
- Loops overshoot their start and drift outward; boxes end a little past the corner they began at.

## Colour logic

- Graphite carries the picture. Terracotta `#d97757` (shade `#b85c3f`) is the hero and only the hero; highlighter yellow `#f7d64a` is the one word to read, or the one glowing object; a red marker `#e0483a` once per scene (a title underline or a stamp); green `#4f9d5d` for a check.
- Real props keep their own small colours (a yellow pencil, a pink eraser, cream tape). A second thing turning terracotta breaks the meaning of the colour.
- Mess is graphite, what is settled is ink: raw input, clutter and first tries are pencil tangles; results are inked, neat and few.

## Type

- Hand lettering from local system hand fonts (a marker face for emphasis), each glyph placed separately with jitter that boils and a pen-weight stroke so a thin hand font reads at phone size. Letters write on one at a time (each fades in, rises a little, grows to full size).
- Captions are short (about 20 characters a line, two lines, big), with ONE highlighter swipe on one word after the line is down. A red underline marks a title.

## Motion

- On 2s. A drawing is written on along its own path with a pencil prop at the tip; rubbing out uses an eraser prop and crumbs. Only a reaction pops: a `!`, a stamp, a hop.
- Quick cuts are steps of one move: at 30 fps and 112 BPM a beat is 16 frames, an 8th 8, a 16th 4; a run of 16th cuts reads as one gesture.
- The hero acts with squash and stretch, a hop on the beat, eyes that look at the subject, blinks, and small graphite marks for feeling (`?`, `!`, sweat, sparkle).

## Camera grammar

- Lean in on the subject, pull back to show the world, always across the same page. A world change is a morph on the bare page, never a cut to somewhere else.

## Signature features

1. Graphite and ink draw nearly everything; one accent colour appears only on the hero, one highlighter on the word to read.
2. Things are written on (pencil, then ink, then colour scribble), not popped in.
3. Every line boils on 2s while its structure stays fixed.
4. The page is always visible: grid, binding, smudge or page edge in every frame.
5. Hand lettering narrates in short captions with one highlighted word.

## When to use

- Explainers about thinking, drafting, planning, learning; a founder-voiced or personal product; anything that benefits from showing the work in progress.
- Short films with no voice-over where the captions carry the story.

Avoid when: slick premium or enterprise brands, dense data, anything that needs polish more than warmth.
