# Retro: synthwave, VHS, Y2K

A period medium aesthetic: neon perspective grid, striped sun, chrome type, VHS tracking glitches, acid Y2K chrome.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

A whole-era quotation. Synthwave is a starry night, striped magenta-to-orange sun, wireframe mountains and a neon grid rushing toward the camera; VHS is scanlines, colour fringing and tape noise; Y2K is liquid chrome, acid green and purple, skeuomorphic system windows and low-fi 3D.

Not: a clean modern gradient look or a generic neon-on-black.

## Materials

- Perspective grid scrolling toward the horizon, a multi-layer sunset sun, a double glow (tight about 4 px plus wide about 30 px), chrome text with multi-band metallic gradients and bevel highlights.
- VHS chain: red/blue channel offset of 1 to 2 px, scanlines (2 px at 10 percent), wave distortion, a 4:3 rounded mask.

## Colour logic

- Deep purple sky, neon magenta and cyan grid, sunset gradient; Y2K acid green and violet with chrome.

## Type

- Chrome or gradient display lettering with glow; pixel or system fonts for Y2K windows.

## Motion

- The grid scrolls by looping vertical displacement; Y2K windows pop up, cursors click and windows drag; deliberately unstable frame rate (key moments dropped to 15 fps or two frames reversed for tape stutter).

## Camera grammar

- Forward drift toward the horizon; title slams with crash hits.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. A one-point perspective neon grid receding to a horizon.
2. A striped sunset sun and double-layer glow.
3. Chrome metallic lettering with bevel highlights.
4. VHS artefacts: RGB split, scanlines, wave glitches, tape jumps.
5. Skeuomorphic Y2K windows or acid chrome when in Y2K mode.

## When to use

- Music, gaming, nostalgia and streetwear brands; retro-tech launches that nod to a decade; an era-specific beat inside a longer film.

Avoid when: Serious enterprise, medical or finance stories; modern minimal brands.
