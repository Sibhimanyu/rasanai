# Halftone paper collage

One flat, bold colour field and three to six black-and-white halftone cut-outs with cream keylines and soft paper shadows, assembled from empty.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style. Numbers are the source's working values (two sibling skills: a B-roll one for single ideas and an information-graphics one built for HyperFrames), summarised in our words.

## Essence, and what it is not

The rule: one idea is one small assembled still life on a flat field. A sentence is squeezed into a single visual proposition (a clock of film strips that yields one short clip; a filing cabinet that sorts a heap into a rule book) and built from three to six objects, never more: past six the meaning weakens. The frame reads as an editorial paper collage, made by hand and lit by one soft lamp.

Not: a mood board, a pile of stickers, a glossy 3D scene, a photo-real room, a vector flat illustration, or a screen with a texture filter on it. Nothing drifts, wobbles or slowly zooms; a frame that lives by a gentle float is another style.

## Materials

- The field: ONE flat, even, saturated colour, no gradient (a gradient breaks the paper at once), with a fine uncoated-paper fibre over everything and a faint 4 px print dot; a very light vignette. These three layers sit on top, never animate, and are the only texture.
- The cut-outs: greyscale photographic pieces (a halftone screen, 4 px at 1080 wide, multiplied) as the skeleton, in the shapes of real things: a machine, a clock, a film strip, a cabinet, a rule book, hands, a chess piece.
- Coloured cardstock for the pieces that carry the message: red, yellow, cyan, orange, purple, cream-white, each serving the reading order, not decoration.
- Every piece has a crisp cut edge, a 2 px warm cream keyline (`#FCF8ED`, the knife's highlight), one soft low-opacity shadow with one direction and one softness for the whole frame, and a hand-set tilt of 0.3 to 1.6 degrees (dead straight reads as a slide deck).
- Card tones for the information-graphic version: main card `#F6F1E4`, secondary `#EAE1CC`, the one accent card in the spot colour. Torn-paper layers (pre-drawn irregular polygons, never a regular zigzag) hold a frame's foot.

## Colour logic

- The field is chosen by MEANING, not by taste (the rule is in `design-desk.md`): burnt orange or red = labour, time spent, urgency; mustard = a tool, a warning, experience leaking away; deep green = cognition, taste, a system reset; deep purple = norms, sediment, long memory; teal = judgement, collaboration, automatic execution. A set of pieces keeps one design language over different fields.
- One spot colour per frame, under 10% of its area, plus one oversized field-coloured disc cropped by the frame as the colour's centre of weight (a whole circle turns into decoration). The four paper-and-spot pairs of the information version: cream `#EEE7D6` with green `#1F5142`; cool grey `#E6E7E3` with ink blue `#18334D`; warm stone `#D8D0BC` with coral `#E0783C`; warm stone with mustard `#D6A92B` for the hook and the close (the first and last frame share a pair, so the film closes its loop).
- Greys are the skeleton, colour is the message: a piece is coloured only when it is where the eye must land.

## Type

- No type baked into the picture: no readable letters, numerals, logos, subtitles or UI in the still. Words are set live on top.
- Information version: Chinese or Latin short lines in a medium weight cut like paper letters (no outline, no shadow), enormous numerals in a bold grotesque with tight tracking (the numeral is the strongest object in this look: let it carry a frame), small monospaced kickers for numbers and units with wide tracking, a header kicker and a short rule at the same spot on every frame.
- Never invent content: a line with no source stays an empty card.

## Motion

- ASSEMBLE FROM EMPTY: the first frame is the bare field (or the field and its structure), then structure arrives first, the subject and cards second, the action and the result last. The last frame IS the approved still (the still is the hold frame; the motion is how that poster was put together).
- Never drift, wobble, float or slowly zoom a finished collage; between arrivals it is still.
- Stop-motion grammar for the stepped version: land on a 10 fps grid (every third frame at 30), eases are `steps(n)`: `steps(3)` for a landing, `steps(4)` for a slide-in, `steps(6)` for a flip, `steps(24)` for a counter. After each important landing, hold at least 0.8 s: a pause is a readable poster.
- Transitions are paper being pulled away, flipped, sucked in or pushed in; opacity is only a 0.1 to 0.2 s helper, never the main transition.
- One main move per piece, never the same one twice in a film: suck-in (summarise), stack-and-land (several units), slam-in (a number), depth-pull (a blur that clears with a counter), grow-from-the-foot (a flow or tree, strictly bottom up), drawer (a library), focus-converge (many to one), flip (before and after).

## Camera grammar

- Locked and flat, straight on; the cut-outs are in a plane with their own tiny shadows, so the depth is only the shadow and the stack order.
- No push, no pan, no parallax. A scale change is an object arriving or being pulled in, not a camera; the nearest to a camera move is a focus-converge on the chosen piece.
- Portrait and landscape both work; in a 9:16 version the information sits in the upper two thirds and the foot is paper and torn edge only.

## Signature features

1. One flat colour field chosen by what the idea means, with no gradient and no second background colour.
2. Three to six objects only, mostly black-and-white halftone cut-outs, with coloured cardstock only on the pieces that carry the message.
3. Every piece has a cut edge, a cream keyline of about 2 px, one shared soft shadow direction and a hand-set tilt under 2 degrees.
4. A paper-fibre and print-dot layer on top of everything, still, and no other texture.
5. The motion is assemble-from-empty on a stepped grid with holds of 0.8 s or more; nothing drifts, wobbles or zooms.
6. One spot colour per frame under 10% of the area, with one oversized cropped disc of the field colour.

## When to use

- A single abstract idea or one claim per beat, B-roll under a voice, the explainer chapter that needs a metaphor made of objects; information beats with a number, a flow, a sort, a before and after.
- Films that want an editorial, tactile, hand-made craft feel, and brands whose look is paper, print or zine.

Avoid when: a screen or UI must be shown exactly (use the real screen), when the subject must be photographed live, when a frame needs more than six things, glow, depth or smooth ease motion, a hero who acts like a character, or text-heavy slides.
