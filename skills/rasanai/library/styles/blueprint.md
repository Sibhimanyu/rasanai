# Blueprint

White technical line work on uneven Prussian blue cyanotype, drawn in drafting order, with annotations that move.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

The whole film happens on one sheet of blueprint paper on a dark drafting table, and the camera reads it like an eye. White line work (centre lines, outlines, hatching, dimension lines, leaders with numbered balloons, a title block, a notes column) is exact and dry; the notes can carry wit. The designed thing is the character and acts through its parts.

Not: a glowing hologram wireframe, a whiteboard, a copperplate engraving, or any 3D render.

## Materials

- Procedural cyanotype in sheet coordinates (two Prussian blues, coating streaks, fibres, creases, bleached edges), on dark drafting cloth with a drop shadow.
- Four line weights; screen width scales with zoom to the power 0.65 so close-ups are not sausages and wides do not vanish; centre, hidden and phantom dash conventions.
- Painter's-order occlusion (fill with paper, then stroke), fine-line hatching at 45 degrees, shadows that darken lines when an object lifts off the paper.

## Colour logic

- Blue and white only; the blue varies in value, the white in weight, never hue.
- One non-blue colour used exactly once: a rubber-stamp ink (classically red) carrying the verdict.
- The optional single 'real thing' at the climax may carry light and soft volume but no new hue.

## Type

- Hand lettering for header, labels and subtitles; a technical sans for title-block fields; a script only for the signature. All written on left to right with a glowing nib.
- Subtitles are drawing notes (double white rules, a NOTE cell) placed in empty sky per shot and also lettered into the GENERAL NOTES column.

## Motion

- Draw-on truncated by arc length with a glowing nib; drafting order: centre lines, outlines, detail, hatching, dimensions, balloons, labels.
- Exploded views on beat subdivisions; mechanisms step on the grid like an escapement; machine acting (sag, wind down, needle overshoot) is the face.

## Camera grammar

- Whole sheet on the table, medium on a figure with sky on one side, track along a path of force, 2x close-up cut on a downbeat, oblique depth factor 0 to 0.5 so the flat drawing 'turns', a section line sweeping across, lateral pan Fig. 1 to Fig. 2, slow push-in with no voice for the one real thing.
- Cuts on downbeats or figure-number rewrites; no dissolves.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Blue-and-white palette on a cyanotype sheet with visible fibres and creases.
2. Linework is drawn on in drafting order with a glowing nib.
3. Dimension lines, numbered balloons and a title block are present on the sheet.
4. Exactly one non-blue stamp, used once, carrying the verdict.
5. Subtitles are lettered drawing notes, not a caption bar.

## When to use

- Engineering, hardware, infrastructure and architecture stories; 'how it works' and teardown films; anything with parts and a verdict.
- A dry-humour or invention-flavoured launch of a mechanism.

Avoid when: People-led stories, consumer lifestyle, warm brands, anything needing photographic imagery.
