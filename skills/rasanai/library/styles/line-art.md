# Line art, one line

A single constant-width line draws, extends and transforms into the next shape; the whole film is one continuous stroke.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

Monochrome (or two-colour) thin line is the only modelling language: it draws itself, extends, turns and becomes the next figure, ending when the line fills into a logo. Lots of negative space and a calm, premium feel.

Not: filled flat illustration, multi-weight sketching, or 3D.

## Materials

- One line weight for the whole film (2 to 4 px), round caps, a softly glowing pen tip leading the draw; older segments dim to about 35 percent as the camera moves on.
- A dark ground with a single metallic or pale line colour.

## Colour logic

- Monochrome or two colours (a midnight ground plus one champagne-gold line is the canonical pairing); a line-to-fill moment is the one colour area.

## Type

- A thin letter-spaced serif or fine sans wordmark that fades up at the end.

## Motion

- Draw-on via stroke dash offset or trim paths; draw speed eases into corners with cubic-bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1).
- The tail of one figure is the start of the next (paths pre-joined); fill expands from the anchor along the same path.

## Camera grammar

- A virtual camera follows the pen tip, drifts or pushes to keep it in frame, then dollies out to show the whole drawing composed as a poster.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Every shape is a constant-width stroke with round caps, with no fills until one deliberate line-to-fill moment.
2. The line draws itself on and the next figure starts at the previous one's end (one continuous path).
3. A glowing pen tip leads the draw and older segments dim.
4. One or two colours only and generous negative space.
5. A camera that follows the tip and pulls out to reveal the whole.

## When to use

- Luxury, architecture, finance and heritage brand films, logo animations, a premium quiet idea told as one transformation chain.

Avoid when: Loud, dense, UI-heavy or comedic films.
