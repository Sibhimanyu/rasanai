# Isometric infographic

A system diagram comes alive: an isometric diorama of flat three-tone blocks, thin ink labels and Isotype numbers.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

Flat, true isometric with no perspective and no outlines on solids; shapes are separated only by the three tones of each face. Infographic furniture (leader lines, label pins, dashed cut lines, hatched section faces, a legend, a route line) lives in the world. Numbers are built from icons and they add up. The system sits on a floating board with a visible cross-section.

Not: low-poly 3D, a flat 2D explainer, or a blueprint.

## Materials

- 30-degree axes with depth-sorted queued drawing; three tones per face (top light, left mid, right dark) derived from one base colour, shadows slightly cool; thin 2 px ink lines only for infographic elements.
- A board floating on a pale ground with a faint isometric dot lattice and a soft shadow, strata visible on its front faces; minimal faceless billboard people stepping at 12 fps.
- Level of detail by zoom so a pull-out never sweeps a giant shape through the frame.

## Colour logic

- A pale neutral ground and five to seven flat hues each with three tones, plus one ink.
- Colour codes meaning like a legend: one hue per material or role, never reused; the subject's hue is reserved and everything else desaturates when the story needs 'this one'.

## Type

- One geometric sans: titles 600 all caps with about 0.12 em tracking, large numbers, units at half size; text carries a ground-colour halo.
- Label pins (dot, leader, rule, text) placed so they never cover the subject; the subtitle is a map-label card with a tiny iso legend cube; an end frame can be the legend.

## Motion

- Nothing moves without a line leading it (route line, leader, cut line before a skin slides away).
- Cutaway: cut line (0.3 s), near skin slides out along its normal (0.35 s), hatched section rims fade in; Isotype icons pop with back-ease one per beat subdivision while count-ups run; accumulation accelerates (quarters, eighths, waves).
- Solids arrive by motion never by alpha; objects squash about 18 percent on landing.

## Camera grammar

- World centre plus log-interpolated zoom; the projection never changes. Follow along a route, hold wide with a circular inset, push through nested cutaways, fast log pull-out for release, locked frame while things accumulate, lateral follow over emptiness, top-to-bottom tilt through strata, switch boards for before/after.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. True 30-degree isometric with three flat tones per face and no outlines on solids.
2. A floating diorama board with a visible cross-section showing strata.
3. Leader lines, label pins, dashed cut lines and a legend live in the world.
4. Numbers are drawn as counted Isotype icons that add up.
5. One hue per meaning, reserved for the subject.

## When to use

- Systems, supply chains, infrastructure, data pipelines and architecture explainers; 'how the whole thing fits together' and scale stories.
- Cloud or platform launches that want a friendly diagram.

Avoid when: Single-hero product reveals, emotional or people-led stories, brands that need photographic or glass finish.
