# Halftone dossier

A retro print case file: cream archive paper, overprinted halftone dots, heavy headlines with misregistered shadows, slamming rubber stamps.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

The whole film is paper. A warm cream sheet with mottling, grain, a vignette and a fold; every shade is halftone dots, never a gradient; two or three dot layers overprint at different screen angles. A bureaucratic apparatus (case numbers, exhibits, name plates, stamps) is the frame, and the subject can be grave or trivial. Loud but readable: one idea per 2 to 4 s card.

Not: a riso zine, Swiss kinetic type on white, or a candy game-show set. Dots, paper and stamps must be visible in every frame.

## Materials

- Paper layer multiplied over everything (mottling, warm noise, vignette, centre fold) plus per-frame dust specks.
- Halftone: a rotated dot grid, radius from a density field (radial, edge, linear), a second layer multiplied so inks overprint; halftone-filled numerals and shapes.
- Line boil on characters and props only (never text, dots or captions); thick-outline chunky characters; rough-edged tilted stamps at about 93 percent opacity.

## Colour logic

- A paper ground, one dark ink that carries all outlines, text and the caption bar, and four or five flat spot inks.
- One ink is the overprint shadow, one the keyword highlight, one warm red reserved for stamps and alarms.
- Flat inks only; night scenes swap to a dark-ink ground with a lighter dot layer.

## Type

- Black-weight serif or slab headlines (100 to 230 px) with a misregistration shadow offset about +8/+8 px in the overprint ink; a fat rounded display face for numbers; a heavy mono for case numbers and dates.
- Cards are the subtitles: a headline per beat plus a dark-ink caption pill with the keyword in the highlight ink.

## Motion

- Transforms at 30 fps with line boil at about 10 fps; everything enters with overshoot (0.3 to 0.45 s), nothing fades in; per-character drop-and-squash headlines.
- Stamp slam: about 0.09 s from 2.6x to 1x, a damped bounce, with camera shake, flash and sound on the same frame.
- Dot-wipe transitions (a coarse circle grid grows to cover and shrinks away, about 0.44 s) or hard cuts on big hits.

## Camera grammar

- A flat frontal page: locked with a 0.6 percent beat pulse, slow push-in for scrutiny, shake with quadratic decay on impact, white flash frames for a revelation, pan across a pinned board, snap zoom into a halftone detail, page or folder slide, pull back to the whole desk.
- Compose like a printed page: headline top-left, subject right-centre, caption bar bottom-centre, HUD in the corners.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Shading is halftone dots overprinted at different angles, never a gradient.
2. Headlines carry a misregistered overprint shadow.
3. Rubber stamps slam with shake, flash and sound on one frame.
4. Paper mottling, dust and a fold are visible in every frame.
5. Scene changes are dot wipes or hard cuts.

## When to use

- Playful or deadpan investigations: a bug report as a case file, a changelog as exhibits, a verdict, a trial of an idea; audiences that enjoy print nostalgia.
- Short punchy cards with one idea each.

Avoid when: Premium, serious or minimal brands, dense UI demos, anything needing glow or depth.
