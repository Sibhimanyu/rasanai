# Aurora gradient and glassmorphism

Large blurred colour blobs drifting under frosted translucent glass cards: slow, translucent, expensive.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

The 2024 to 2026 AI-product launch default: a deep-ground aurora mesh gradient of three to five adjacent-hue blobs drifting on long paths, with frosted glass cards floating at parallax depths and a Liquid-Glass lens that refracts and magnifies. Nothing is fast; text enters with opacity and an 8 px rise only.

Not: hard-edged flat colour, a fast energetic reel, or mixed clashing hues (they turn muddy).

## Materials

- Aurora layer: 3 to 5 solid circles blurred 80 to 150 px on Bezier loops of 20 to 40 s, blended into a mesh gradient with 3 to 5 percent noise against banding.
- Glass card recipe: backdrop blur 20 to 40 px plus 5 to 10 percent white fill plus a 1 px 30 percent white inner highlight plus a big radius; an SDF refractive lens with chromatic dispersion on its rim.

## Colour logic

- Two or three adjacent hues (violet, blue, cyan or orange, pink, red) on a dark base; bright blobs on dark beat bright grounds.

## Type

- A light elegant sans; titles breathe in with opacity plus small lift.

## Motion

- Near-linear or sine breathing easing everywhere; fast moves destroy the mood.
- Cards float with parallax and slight 3D tilt; micro-UI inside animates by opacity and 8 px rise.

## Camera grammar

- Slow drifts and parallax; the lens glides across and magnifies.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Slowly drifting, heavily blurred colour blobs form the background.
2. Frosted glass cards with a 1 px highlight edge and large radius.
3. Two or three neighbouring hues only; no clashing colours.
4. Everything moves slowly on sine or near-linear easing.
5. A fine noise layer prevents banding.

## When to use

- AI and software launches, assistants, consumer apps with a calm premium promise; a hero or closing scene behind the product UI.

Avoid when: Fast, funny or tactile stories; brands with strict flat palettes; print-like looks.
