# Liquid motion

Shapes flow, stretch, drip and merge like thick glossy paint; a logo condenses from a drop.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

Organic, glossy, high-energy liquid: wave-edged transitions that wash over the screen, metaballs that fuse, strings that stretch and snap, and secondary droplets that follow the main mass. Reads as thick 3D liquid with specular highlights and a fresnel rim, not flat blobs.

Not: flat vector blobs, a particle cloud, or straight geometric moves.

## Materials

- A WebGL SDF metaball field with smooth-min and fake 3D lighting from the field gradient, or a blur plus alpha-threshold trick for sticky merging; a glossy candy gradient on a deep ground.

## Colour logic

- A deep (plum) ground with one glossy hot-to-cool candy gradient (orange to pink to violet).

## Type

- Wordmark formed by the liquid; supporting type minimal and bold.

## Motion

- Ease is flung, never uniform (about cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1)); a stretch-snap-rebound on separation (a thin neck, then two to three frames of rebound each side).
- Secondary droplets trail the mass by 2 to 3 frames and squash on landing.

## Camera grammar

- Mostly locked or slow; the liquid is the camera move.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. A wavy leading edge wipe with 3 to 5 phase-offset bulges.
2. Metaball merging (shapes fuse when close).
3. Stretch, neck, snap and rebound on separation.
4. Secondary droplets that follow the main mass late.
5. No straight lines: every curve is organic.

## When to use

- Beverage, beauty, music and youth brands; logo genesis; energetic transitions between sections.

Avoid when: Rigid, technical or minimal brands; film-long use without relief.
