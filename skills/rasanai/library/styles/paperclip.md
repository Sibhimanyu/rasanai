# Bold-outline sticker explainer

Photo cut-outs turned stickers with thick white outlines, flat icons and exact charts on one giant canvas with a continuous camera.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

The knowledge-channel standard: product photos are cut out and given a thick white outline so they read as stickers, laid with flat icons and precise charts on a huge canvas, with the camera panning and zooming through information points. Every sentence brings a new visual element; charts are exact; colour is restrained for a serious tone.

Not: decorative illustration, a slide deck, or a loud palette.

## Materials

- A very wide canvas (e.g. 6000x3000 px) with a camera transform; cut-out photos with 8 to 12 px white outlines and a soft shadow to unify sources; flat icons; exact charts (bars to scale, consistent numbers).

## Colour logic

- Calm blue-grey and off-white canvas with slate, 2 to 3 colours plus one accent.

## Type

- Equal-weight sans with strict alignment, a heavy sans and huge number for the closing title card.

## Motion

- Charts grow and count up, route paths draw on; narration drives each element's entrance; long-ease camera pans and zooms string many points in one take.

## Camera grammar

- One continuous long-eased camera path across a giant canvas, ending in a pull-out to a bold title card.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Photos are cut out and given a thick uniform white outline (sticker look).
2. A calm blue-grey and off-white canvas with 2 to 3 colours and one accent.
3. Exact charts: bars to scale, counted-up numbers, drawn routes.
4. One continuous camera path across a giant canvas.
5. Every spoken sentence introduces a new visual element.

## When to use

- Knowledge, finance and corporate explainers; supply-chain or 'what is inside' stories; dense but orderly information.

Avoid when: Mood films, emotional pieces, luxury or abstract brand work.
