# Flat vector 2D

The canonical explainer look: solid colour shapes, geometric characters, elastic entrances, one continuous flat world.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

Pure vector shapes in bold colour blocks with no gradients, where one hero moves at a time with an elastic anticipation, stretch, squash and settle. The camera pushes through one continuous flat world and transitions are carried by objects (a shape wipe, an element flying out).

Not: gradient-heavy UI glass, hand-drawn or boiling line work, or 3D.

## Materials

- Flat fills, no or minimal outline, geometric characters rigged with anchor-point limbs, clean negative space.
- A bold 5 to 6 colour high-saturation palette of one ground, one ink and several brights.

## Colour logic

- Big colour blocks and collisions of saturated brand colours; no gradients or very few; ink for contrast.

## Type

- A friendly geometric or rounded sans, heavy for titles; text enters with the same elastic move as shapes.

## Motion

- Elastic easing is the soul: overshoot (about cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1)) with a three-phase anticipation, burst and rebound.
- Stagger 2 to 4 frames, only one hero moving at a time, about 10 percent squash and stretch on moves, 30 fps.
- Transitions: a shape wipe or an element that flies out and carries the cut.

## Camera grammar

- One continuous take pushing through the world; a push into a window, a notification bubble expanding to fill the frame as the wipe into the next section.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Solid colour blocks with no (or almost no) gradients and no heavy outlines.
2. Elastic overshoot entrances with anticipation, stretch and squash on movers.
3. Elements stagger in by 2 to 4 frames and only one hero moves at a time.
4. Transitions are carried by a shape wipe or a flying element, never a dissolve.
5. A saturated 5 to 6 colour palette with generous clean space.

## When to use

- SaaS and app explainers, onboarding, feature announcements, anything friendly and clear; the safe-but-polished base to blend a second idiom into.

Avoid when: Luxury, gritty, technical-serious or glass-and-light launches.
