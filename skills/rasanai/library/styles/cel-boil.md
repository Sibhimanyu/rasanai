# Cel animation and line boil

Hand-made frame-by-frame magic: ink linework that re-draws every two frames, smear frames and hand-drawn FX on a paper ground.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

A hybrid of clean shapes and hand-drawn cel effects (smoke, sparks, star bursts, speed lines) in the Buck and Giant Ant tradition, animated mostly on twos. Lines boil (slightly redrawn each pair of frames) and fills sit slightly off-register like hand colouring, on warm paper.

Not: smooth digital vector, perfect symmetry, or a filter laid over static art.

## Materials

- Warm paper ground with 10 to 15 percent multiplied grain; variable-width ink linework; fills slightly off-register.
- A 3 to 4 colour palette (ink black, tomato red, sunny yellow, cream).

## Colour logic

- Three or four flat colours plus paper; no gradients.

## Type

- Hand-lettered titles that keep boiling on the final hold.

## Motion

- On twos (12 fps effective) with fast action on ones and holds on threes; boil by cycling 3 to 4 jittered variants every 2 frames (turbulent displace plus posterized time).
- Smear frames stretch a mover to 150 to 300 percent on one frame; cel FX drawn frame by frame over the main animation.

## Camera grammar

- Simple, mostly locked frames so the drawing carries the energy.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Line boil: outlines are redrawn with small variation every 2 frames.
2. Animation on twos, fast action on ones and holds on threes.
3. Smear frames on fast moves.
4. Hand-drawn smoke, spark and burst FX over the main art.
5. A visible paper grain multiplied over everything.

## When to use

- Crafted, human, warm brands; fun launch beats and mascots; stories about making things by hand; a break from digital polish.

Avoid when: Precision or enterprise brands, dense UI, high-end tech.
