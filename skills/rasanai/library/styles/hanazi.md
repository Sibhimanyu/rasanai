# Variety-show kinetic captions (hanazi)

Emotional decorative captions with multi-layer outlines, elastic pops and hand-drawn burst, sweat and sparkle marks.

A reference for the design desk to blend one or two traits from, never a preset that replaces the film's bespoke system. Never cite it as the whole look; the signature features below are what a blend must keep if it names this style.

## Essence, and what it is not

Captions descended from TV variety-show post-production, now the common packaging language for vlogs, pet videos and comedic edits: rounded heavy faces with a thick white stroke, a coloured outer stroke and a drop shadow pop in on the joke, change colour and shape with the person's mood, and carry doodle decorations.

Not: quiet lower-thirds, elegant editorial captions, or captions that appear before the moment.

## Materials

- Three-layer type: fill, thick white stroke, coloured outer stroke or shadow; SVG burst boxes with speed lines, sweat drops and sparkles; a footage-like base with slow Ken Burns, light handheld shake and snap punch-ins.

## Colour logic

- High-saturation pink, yellow and cyan with white strokes.

## Type

- Rounded, fat display faces; tilted hand-lettered for complaint, radial burst boxes for shock, a small bracketed aside for whispered lines.

## Motion

- Pop in as scale 0 to 1.15 to 1 in about 8 frames or per-character bounce with a 2-frame stagger; appear 2 to 3 frames after the audio moment; wiggle slightly while held.

## Camera grammar

- Punch-in zooms on 'moments' over slow Ken Burns.

## Signature features

Checkable traits; the frame juror and the critic look for each by name when a system claims this style.

1. Fat rounded type with a white stroke, a coloured outer stroke and a shadow.
2. An elastic pop-in (0 to 1.15 to 1) timed to sound hits.
3. Mood-matched decoration (burst boxes, sweat drops, sparkles).
4. High-saturation pink, yellow and cyan on white strokes.
5. Captions arrive slightly after the picture and sound moment.

## When to use

- Social-first, comedic, creator or pet-style edits; a reactive caption layer over real footage; casual consumer brands.

Avoid when: Premium, serious or minimal brands; formal explainers.
