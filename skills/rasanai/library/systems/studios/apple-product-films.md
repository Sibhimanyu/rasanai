## What it is
Apple's product film language is a restrained, high-craft register: a product on a controlled stage, slow lit camera moves, short sentence-case claims in SF Pro, and (for the 1999-2019 design films) a calm voiceover from Jony Ive about materials and process. Apple publishes no motion bible, so this entry combines what Apple does publish (type system, web CSS tokens, the Scary Fast production notes) with clearly marked observation. The Scary Fast event (October 30, 2023, shot on iPhone 15 Pro Max) is the documented production example.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- **Type is a system with numbers.** Apple's web stylesheet (fetched) sets headlines at weight 600, line-height 1.05 to 1.08, with tracking that relaxes as size falls (-0.015em at 80px, -0.009em at 64px, -0.003em at 48px) and body at weight 400, 17px/1.47, -0.022em. Apple's fonts page: SF Pro has nine weights, variable optical sizes, four widths; "size-specific outlines and dynamic tracking". San Francisco was released to developers on November 18, 2014 (Wikipedia). Proprietary licence: use Inter for open work.
- **Stage greys are real values.** #000 and #f5f5f7 for dark stages and text on dark, #1d1d1f ink on light, #86868b and #6e6e73 for secondary text. Interface blue #0071e3 belongs to links and buttons, not to films.
- **The film is shot like cinema.** Scary Fast: iPhone 15 Pro Max, 4K60 ProRes in Apple Log, Blackmagic Camera app, SpaceCam rig, dollies, cranes and drones, Beastgrip cages, Tentacle Sync timecode, director Brian Oakes, colourist Stefan Sonnenfeld, Apple Park at night, edited on a Mac (Apple Newsroom). Warm tungsten over deep black is what its frames measure as.
- **One idea per beat; the rest is stage.** An observed pattern, not a stated rule.
- **Material first.** Macro of aluminium, glass or titanium before the whole object appears.
- Voiceover of design intent (Ive over machining shots) is reported in secondary summaries only; not fetched, treat as unverified.

## Tokens decoded
- Stage: #000000 or #ffffff. Text: #f5f5f7 on black, #1d1d1f on white. Secondary line: #86868b on black, #6e6e73 on white.
- Hero line: Inter 600 (variable `wght` 600), cap height-driven size 6 to 9 percent of frame height (proposed; Apple's 80px on a 1440 wide page is 5.6 percent of width), line-height 1.05, tracking -0.015em for the largest sizes, -0.022em for 17 to 24 px equivalents. Secondary line Inter 400 at 2.6 to 3 percent.
- Margins 8 to 10 percent; hero line optically centred.
- Shadows: contact only, blur 24 to 40 px, 25 to 35 percent black, y offset 8 to 16 (proposed).
- Saturation budget: product colour only; no wash over 15 percent opacity.

## Motion and camera
2D (HyperFrames/GSAP), timings partly sourced: Apple's CSS uses `opacity 400ms ease-in-out`, `transform .3s cubic-bezier(0,0,0.5,1)` and `opacity 100ms linear` (fetched). For film beats (proposed): headline lines `y: 16, opacity: 0` to rest in 700 ms `power3.out`, 100 ms stagger between lines, read 1.8 s on a slow carry, fade out 300 ms `power2.in`. Line by line, never letter by letter. UI push: `scale 1 to 1.06` over 1.2 s `power2.inOut`. Per vocabulary.md section 7, treat this as "restrained: one move per beat". Grade: lift blacks to nothing, warm key, no teal-orange.
3D (Rasan3D, per references/3d.md section 5): stage with `environment: "studio"`, `toneMapping: "neutral"` so brand colours hold, `k.rig("top-soft", { dir: [-0.4, 0.9, 0.5], shadowSoftness: 10 })` for a large soft key; `k.material("brushed-metal")` for aluminium, `"glass"` for the display with an opaque `background: "#000"`; a floor with a faint reflection. Camera keys: `pos` one slow dolly of 10 to 20 percent over 5 s with `"sine.inOut"`; `lens` keyed 85 to 100 mm for macro and 35 mm for the hero; `fstop: 2.8` with `focus: "target"` for macro, then a `fstop` key rising toward 8 as the pull-out reveals the whole device. Move the light, not the object: animate the rig key direction across the surface for 1.5 to 2.5 s in `pose(t, k)`. Exploded views: parts offset along one axis by 0.3 to 0.6 of part height, 800 to 1200 ms `power3.inOut`, offset 40 ms per part. Labels: `k.pinDom(el, { at: part, width, face: "camera", px: [w, h] })` so type stays crisp DOM. Bloom only on one `emissive` indicator light (intensity 2 to 3); nothing else glows. Post: `grain: 0.015`, no `ca`.

## How to instruct a model to build it
Paste-ready: "Apple product film language. One product, one idea per shot. Stage #000000 (or #ffffff), text #f5f5f7 (or #1d1d1f), secondary #86868b. Type Inter 600, hero lines 6 to 9 percent of frame height, line-height 1.05, tracking -0.015em, sentence case, at most 7 words, one secondary Inter 400 line. Lines enter with a 16 px rise and opacity in 700 ms power3.out, stagger 100 ms, read 1.8 s on a slow carry, fade 300 ms. Camera: one slow dolly per shot (10 to 20 percent over 5 s sine.inOut), macro start at 85 to 100 mm f/2.8 that pulls back to the whole product; the light moves, not the product. Soft contact shadow only. The only saturated colour is the product. No bounce, no whoosh stock, no lens flare, no gradient backdrop." Claude tends to add too many animated elements per beat; limit each beat to one motion. GPT tends to add gradient backdrops and glass cards; forbid them in the brief.

## Blending notes
Carries: restraint, size-dependent tracking, one-idea beats, light-driven cameras. Mixes with ManvsMachine for CG polish, Swiss grids for data, Linear/Vercel for software UI. Breaks against anything that needs visible texture, noise or hand-made irregularity. Also clashes with noisy grunge and maximalist collage (not library entries).
