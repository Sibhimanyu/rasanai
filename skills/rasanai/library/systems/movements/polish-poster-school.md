## What it is
A postwar Polish poster tradition, running from the late 1940s to the 1980s, in which designers acted as authors rather than advertisers. MoMA describes it as a "sophisticated visual language characterized by surreal and expressionist tendencies, a bold use of color, and macabre, often satirical humor", made mostly for opera, theatre, film and exhibitions at a time of "little or no advertising", under a censoring state that nevertheless tolerated oblique criticism. Tomaszewski (1914-2005), who started reworking film posters in 1947-48 and taught the poster department at the Warsaw Academy from 1952, is credited as founder; the 1966 Biennale and the 1968 Poster Museum at Wilanow made it an institution.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- One metaphor per sheet. The image is the reading of the film, not a scene from it (illustrationhistory.org on Trepkowski: the artist draws on the viewer's "associative capacities").
- Colour is a field. In all four measured Tomaszewski posters one flat ground takes 40-57% of the sheet (yellow #e5c706, ultramarine #3f73cf, indigo #36396e, near-black #2f2f2e), with one or two small earth or paper tones (#dbcba5, #ac825f, #9e6965) and a dark ink.
- Lettering is drawn and secondary. Hand lettering is "integrated into designs as interpretive elements, not typeset" (illustrationhistory.org).
- Personal hands, not a house style: Lenica (cut-out, geometric, an animator; MoMA: his Wozzeck, 1964, "uses a psychedelic aesthetic"), Starowieyski (macabre, erotic, dense; first Polish solo show at MoMA, 1985), Cieslewicz (collage, Paris later), Swierzy (bold colour spots, a Cyrk poster almost every week per illustrationhistory.org), Trepkowski (symbolic reduction, 'Nie!' 1952).
- Violence and satire arrive as image, never as text. The poster says it sideways because the censor read words.
- Technique is print: lithography, then screenprint and offset; painted gouache originals; airbrush came from the Gronowski/Deco lineage.

## Tokens decoded
- Canvas = the ground field. Pick one: #e5c706 (yellow), #3f73cf (ultramarine), #36396e (indigo), #2f2f2e (black). Second tone #dbcba5 paper. Ink #232938. Accent small: #9e6965 or #ac825f, under 8% of area. Measured from Tomaszewski prints; any red or teal is proposed, not measured.
- Display lettering: SVG paths, or "Caveat Brush" 400 at 120-200px (about 11-18% of frame height) with `rotate(-3deg)` and uneven letter offsets of 0-6px; "Amatic SC" 700 for cut-paper capitals. Body "Work Sans" 400/500 at 20-24px, one line, in a corner. Do not tween `wght` on the lettering (it is not meant to be a system).
- Composition: metaphor silhouette 60-80% of frame height, ground to all four edges, text under 8% of area.
- Texture: 3-6% paper-tooth grain, per-frame stepped (see below), not a full-frame distress overlay.

## Motion and camera
2D (HyperFrames HTML/CSS/GSAP; vocabulary.md camera and texture terms):
- Cut-out stepping: each moving part gets `ease: "steps(N)"` where N = duration x 12 (a 0.5 s move = `steps(6)`), the cut-out animation feel Lenica worked in (Wikipedia: "cut-out stop motion animation"). Entrance: x or y from -14% of frame, 0.4-0.5 s, rotation 4 to 0 degrees stepped, `power2.out` for the one smooth hero element.
- Metaphor morph: two stacked SVG silhouettes, the second under a clip-path (`inset`/`circle`) that opens 0 to 100% over 0.7 s `power2.inOut`; the ground colour tweens at the same time (0.7 s `sine.inOut`), e.g. #e5c706 to #232938.
- Hold the finished composition 1.0-1.8 s with a 3% world scale drift over 6 s (`sine.inOut`) so it lives without moving.
- Transitions: hard cut, or a ground-colour wipe (the next ground slides in as a flat block, 0.35 s `power3.inOut`). No dissolves, no whip.
- Grain: a 256 px noise tile with `background-position` stepped per frame at 12 fps, 3-6% opacity, multiply.
3D (Rasan3D, per references/3d.md section 3, 5, 15):
- Layers as paper: `k.svg(svgText, { width, depth: 0.02, bevel: 0, material: k.material("paper", { color }) })` for each silhouette; 4-6 layers spaced 0.15-0.4 m on z; ground a `k.pxPlane` or a large plane at the back in the ground colour.
- Camera: `lens: [[0, 35]]`, pos a slow truck `[[0,[-0.3,0,6]],[6,[0.3,0,6],"sine.inOut"]]`, target fixed; `fstop: 5.6` only if the nearest layer should soften; otherwise leave it out so every layer stays crisp like print.
- Light: `k.rig("window", { dir: [-0.6, 0.8, 1], shadows: true, shadowSoftness: 3 })` so each paper layer throws a short hard-ish shadow on the one behind (this is the whole 3D read).
- Look: `post: { grain: 0.03, vignette: 0.15 }` and `toneMapping: "neutral"`; no bloom. Optional post pass `k.pass("ink", { at: "post", frag })` using `r3Dither(c, gl_FragCoord.xy, 5.0)` to posterise toward print.
- Type stays DOM (`k.pinDom` for lettering that must ride a layer).

## How to instruct a model to build it
"Polish poster school. One central visual metaphor for the whole film, composed as a silhouette filling 70% of frame height on one flat ground field that covers about half the frame (#e5c706, #3f73cf, #36396e or #2f2f2e; second tone #dbcba5; ink #232938; accent under 8%). Hand-drawn lettering as SVG, one to three words, under 8% of the frame, never centred perfectly. Moving parts on `steps(n)` at 12 fps with 0.4-0.5 s entrances and a 4 degree rotation settle; one silhouette morphs into another under a clip-path over 0.7 s `power2.inOut` while the ground colour changes; hold the finished poster 1.2 s. Hard cuts only. 3-6% stepped paper grain. No gradients, no stock icons, no drop shadows, no perfectly set type." Claude and GPT models both drift toward adding secondary icons and a gradient sky; state "exactly one metaphor, flat ground, no gradient" as a negative list in the scene's build block.

## Blending notes
Carries: the single metaphor, the flat ground that takes half the frame, drawn lettering, stepped cut-out motion. Works under Swiss type (a rigid sans caption grid beside a Polish image panel), with constructivist diagonals, with Push Pin's illustrated ideas, and with Cuban OSPAAAL screenprint for politics. Breaks with Memphis pattern noise and anything glossy. The honest limit: this is a way of thinking (idea first) more than a palette; if the blend has several visual ideas it has stopped being this.
