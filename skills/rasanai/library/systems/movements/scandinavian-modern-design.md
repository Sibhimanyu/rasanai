## What it is
A movement defined by simplicity, minimalism and functionality that emerged in the early 20th century and flourished in the 1950s across Denmark, Finland, Norway, Sweden and Iceland. Aalto, Jacobsen, Wegner and Poul Henningsen are its usual names; the 1954 "Design in Scandinavia" exhibition at the Brooklyn Museum launched Scandinavian Modern in the American market. National flavours differ: Danish functionalism and craft, Finnish range across materials, Norwegian minimalism. It has two registers that a film should choose between: the quiet wood-and-daylight object (Aalto, Wegner) and the loud flat print (Marimekko).

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- Function first, softened by natural form. Aalto's Stool 60 (1933) answers a structural question: an L-shaped leg of solid birch bent by sawing the end into a fan and gluing veneer into the cuts; the seat tops were made from production offcuts (PIN-UP). The form is the construction.
- Local natural material, visibly worked: birch, beech, oak. The Wishbone Chair (Wegner 1949, Carl Hansen & Son 1950) is turned, waxed beech with a seat of braided paper cord (Nationalmuseet record on Commons). Show the grain and the joint.
- Industrial method, not craft nostalgia: Aalto departed from the tubular-steel line of Le Corbusier and Mies by working with bent wood and local supply (PIN-UP).
- Restraint in colour, then one confident accent. The other pole is Marimekko: Unikko (Maija Isola, 1964) is a gestural poppy painted with a large brush on huge paper laid on the print table, made against founder Armi Ratia's ban on florals (Kotona); Jokapoika (Vuokko Nurmesniemi, 1956) is red and white stripe.
- The Marimekko wordmark (Helge Mether-Borgström, 1954) is "simple and timeless", plain.
- Domestic, human scale; durable, repairable objects.
- Daylight as the primary light is a widely repeated rationale (long dark winters) that none of the fetched pages states; treat it as a design choice, not a fact.

## Motion and camera
All timings are proposals; none of the sources is a moving image.
2D (HyperFrames HTML/CSS/GSAP): `gsap.from(el,{y:16,opacity:0,duration:.7,ease:"power2.out"})`, stagger 0.1s; a pattern repeat (Unikko-like) slides as one sheet `x: 120 to 0`, 1.2s `sine.inOut`; long reads (one slow mover, never frozen) 1.5-3s; transitions are 600ms crossfades or matching-tone push slides (no cuts to black, no whips). Camera: a 6% scale-in over 8s `sine.inOut` on `#world` with `transform-origin` on the object, or a lateral drift of 20-40px/s. Window light is a gradient: from left `rgba(255,248,235,.22)` to `rgba(35,38,43,.10)` on the far side (vocabulary: Window light), shadow soft under the object (ellipse, height 12-18% of width). Print mode (Marimekko): flat #d73044 on #f9f9f9, shapes drawn by `stroke-dashoffset` brush-like paths 900ms `power2.inOut`, no gradients.
3D (Rasan3D): a daylit room. `Rasan3D.stage({ environment:"soft", toneMapping:"neutral", post:{ grain:0.015, vignette:0.15 }, camera:{ pos:Rasan3D.orbit({...}) (below), target:[[0,[0,0.4,0]]], lens:[[0,70]], fstop:2.8 }, ... })`; in `build(k)`: `k.rig("window",{ dir:[-1,0.7,0.5], intensity:1, shadows:true, shadowSoftness:10 })`, `k.ground({ y:0, color:"#e6ded1", shadowOpacity:0.3 })`, the object in `k.material("matte",{ color:"#c4af8c" })` or `k.material("ceramic",{ color:"#f9f9f9" })`; a wood grain texture through `k.image(url)` on the material map when a real photo exists; a GLB via `await k.addon("loaders/GLTFLoader.js")`. Camera: `camera.pos: Rasan3D.orbit({ center:[0,0.4,0], radius:3, height:1.2, from:-20, to:20, t0:0, t1:6, ease:"sine.inOut" })`; never faster than about 5 degrees per second; hold on each angle 1.5s.

## How to instruct a model to build it
"Scandinavian modern. Ground #f9f9f9, wall #e6ded1, wood #c4af8c, #d2a771 and #765c40 as visible grain, ink #23262b, one accent #d73044 used once per scene. Jost 400/500 lowercase, small, line height 1.5, 10% margins; one object per frame at 35-50% of frame height. Soft daylight from the left, shadow `0 24px 48px rgba(35,38,43,.10)`. Motion: 12-20px rise, 700ms power2.out, 1.5-3s holds, 600ms crossfades, no bounce, no whips. No gradients as decoration, no glow, no plastic gloss, no clutter. Type contrast at least 4.5:1." Claude tends to stay on-brand but pale: make the accent decisive and give the wood real grain. GPT-style image passes lean to beige on beige (observation from earlier runs, not measured); require a defined accent and one named object.

## Blending notes
Carries: daylight, wood and matte materials, calm timing, single accent. With Swiss: precise grid plus warm palette is a humane Swiss. With Muji and IKEA: the retail face of the same restraint. With Isotype: soft icons on paper. With paper-cut: the Marimekko print side only. Breaks: neon, glitch, aggressive crops, hard cuts.
