## What it is
Two related looks. Vaporwave is an internet aesthetic and music genre from the early 2010s: Wikipedia places its blueprint in Daniel Lopatin's Chuck Person's Eccojams Vol. 1 (2010) and Ramona Langley's (Macintosh Plus) Floral Shoppe (2011), and lists its imagery as 1990s web design with glitch effects, Memphis Group shapes, Windows 95 imagery, VHS degradation, GeoCities-style graphics, 1980s-90s anime, fullwidth text and stylised Greco-Roman statues, in pastel and neon pink, purple and cyan. Web nostalgia is the underlying material: GeoCities (founded November 1994, renamed December 1995, 29 neighbourhoods by 1996, bought by Yahoo for $3.57 billion in 1999, closed in the US on 26 October 2009) and Windows 95 (a teal desktop with beveled 3D elements, MS Sans Serif, taskbar and Start menu, Brian Eno's startup sound). Treat them as one system with two registers: system chrome, and pastel dream.

## The rules that make it this and not something else
- The source material is real: actual Windows-95-style windows, bevels and dialogs, not a soft generic "retro" UI.
- Windows 95 chrome is flat colour plus bevel: silver `#C0C0C0` face, white top-left highlight, dark grey bottom-right, black outer line, navy `#000080` title bar, teal `#008080` desktop (Web colours entry confirms these named values; the Win95 entry confirms the teal desktop and bevels).
- Vaporwave adds a second register of pastel pink, cyan and violet, classical statuary, Japanese text, palms, sunsets and checkerboards.
- It is ambiguous: sources describe it as both critique and celebration of consumer culture, so its tone is deadpan, not cheery.
- Lo-fi processing, such as VHS softness and a little glitch, is a trait; slowed motion and long reads (never a frozen frame).
- Homepages add 90s web material: tiled backgrounds, marquee text, counters, under-construction signs, neighbourhood names. (GeoCities' neighbourhood structure is sourced; the visual cliches are common knowledge, not from the fetched page.)

## Tokens decoded
- Win95 palette (sourced from the web colour table, which lists the 16 VGA colours; a VM screenshot measured silver #bfc6c8 and title blue #0101a8, so real captures drift from the named values): silver `#C0C0C0`, gray `#808080`, white `#FFFFFF`, black `#000000`, navy `#000080`, teal `#008080`, plus lime `#00FF00`, aqua `#00FFFF`, fuchsia `#FF00FF`, yellow `#FFFF00`, red `#FF0000`.
- Vaporwave pastels (proposed; a representative artwork measured pink #df78d8, violet #7d52c5, sky #75b2ec on night #0a0722): pink `#ff71ce`, cyan `#01cdfe`, mint `#05ffa1`, violet `#b967ff`, sunset yellow `#fffb96`.
- Bevel: `border:2px solid; border-color:#fff #808080 #808080 #fff; box-shadow: inset -1px -1px #000, inset 1px 1px #dfdfdf`. Pressed: swap.
- Title bar: `linear-gradient(90deg,#000080,#1084d0)` (the Win98 gradient; for 95 use flat `#000080`), white bold 11px-equivalent text (scaled 3x in a 1080p composition: 33 px), three 16x14 buttons at the right.
- Type: Pixelify Sans or VT323 for UI text, no smoothing; Noto Sans JP 700 for Japanese; headline in fullwidth Latin.
- Layout: windows at 24 px offsets overlapping; desktop icons 32x32 scaled 3x with 2-line labels in white on teal; taskbar 28 px tall (scaled), Start button at left.

## Motion and camera
2D: Windows 95 had no window animation, so a window should cut in: opacity 0 to 1 in one frame (`steps(1)`), with a 2-frame outline stretch if you want the Win3.x-style "wireframe drag". The cursor moves in straight segments between click targets, 300-500 ms `power1.inOut`, pausing 150 ms before the click; click depress is a 1-frame bevel swap. Vaporwave layers move slowly: bust rotation 8 degrees per second, sun rising 40 px over 6 s `sine.inOut`, checkerboard scrolling at 40 px/s. Marquee at 120 px/s `none` is the exception where linear is the point. For the cut-in window and cursor click, set `data-finish-blur="off"` on that scene's frame root so the film finish does not average the steps; use `steps(n)` and hold frames, never `power*` tweens on the stepped layer. Reads of 1.5-2.5 s, with the cursor or a window still acting. The Eno startup chime is 6 seconds long (sourced); an audio hook if you choose to cut to it, but do not reuse the actual sound (copyright; use an original sound alike).

3D (Rasan3D reading):
- Bust: a GLB from the project (`const {GLTFLoader}=await k.addon("loaders/GLTFLoader.js")`) in `k.material('ceramic', {color:'#f2eef0'})`, lit by `k.rig("three-point",{key:"#ff71ce",fill:"#9a8cff",rim:"#01cdfe",intensity:1,shadows:true})` (`key`, `fill`, `rim` are colours; `intensity` is the one number), `environment {preset:'soft', intensity:.5}`.
- Room: `k.ground({color:'#ffffff'})` checker texture via `k.image`; a back wall with a Windows 95 `k.panel` window (texture from a DOM-rendered screenshot, unlit) at a slight angle; a sun disc as `emissive` 2.5.
- Lens 50 mm for the room, 85 mm on the bust; `fstop` 2.8; slow 3% push over 4 s `sine.inOut`; the bust turns 25 degrees and holds, no constant spin (the stated 3D rule).
- Post: `bloom {strength:.3}`, `grain .03`, `ca 1`, plus the VHS pass from vhs-analog at low strength.
- Avoid: neon grid void and palm silhouettes with no room.

## Blending notes
- Carries: Win95 bevels, teal and silver, pastel second register, fullwidth type, slowness.
- With vhs-analog: vaporwave's source material is tape; run VHS softness at 20-30%. With y2k-chrome or frutiger-aero: pair for a "2000s" timeline, but keep one era per scene. With pixel-game-ui: icons and fonts.
- Breaks: Swiss precision, cassette-futurism's hardware grit (different decade, different mood).
